                                    SETTING

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The fabric of reality has been torn to shreds. It happened imperceptibly
    at first, and then all at once. A  total reality collapse event is what
    reporters called it before all broadcasts cut off.

    A man goes to bed with his wife; he wakes up next to a fish. A truck
    driver barrels down the highway, and a second later the truck and he have
    switched places. Cats bark now. Dogs have forgotten their language.
    Mirrors show rooms that were never there.

    No one knows quite why this happened. Many have their ideas, but ideas
    aren't worth much now that you can't trust the thoughts in your head.

    The chaos left the world a shifting wasteland, but this blank slate may
    be an opportunity in disguise. In the vacuum left after the old order,
    opportunistic warlords have sprung up across the land. There is no return
    to normalcy. No negotiations. No peace in this new world. There is only
    a scramble for whatever resources remain, a scramble to acquire the only
    thing still solid in this world:

                                     Power.

================================================================================
                          QUICKSTART: HOW TO PLAY
================================================================================

    Choose a rules level:

        BASIC GOOSKLERF
            Use only the Core Rules.

        ADVANCED GOOSKLERF
            Use Core Rules plus all Advanced Rules.

        CUSTOM GOOSKLERF
            Use Core Rules plus any Advanced Rules chosen before setup.

    SETUP

        1. Choose a game mode.
        2. Choose a deck size: Small, Medium, or Large.
        3. Each player builds a legal deck.
        4. Each player draws 7 cards.
        5. No cards begin on the table in Classic Mode.
        6. Determine the first player. Play proceeds clockwise.

    TABLE ZONES

        The battlefield is the shared center zone. Entities on the
        battlefield can fight enemy battlefield entities and assault
        enemy fortresses.

        The suburbs are each player's fortress zone. Fortresses are
        placed in the suburbs. Entities inside fortresses can defend,
        fire Initial Volleys when legal, and use Fortress Barrage if
        active. They cannot capture enemy fortresses while inside a
        fortress.

    TURN ORDER

        Each turn has five phases:

            1. CARD PLAY
                Play exactly 3 cards if possible. If fewer than 3 can be
                played, discard until played + discarded = 3.

            2. COMBAT
                Declare and resolve engagements. Each entity may
                participate in only one engagement per turn unless a
                card says otherwise.

            3. MOVEMENT
                Reposition your entities and transfer your own items.
                End-of-Movement positions determine next turn's options.

            4. CARD DRAW
                Draw the top 2 cards of your deck, then search your deck
                for 1 card. Add all 3 to hand, then shuffle.

            5. VICTORY CHECK
                Check Last Man Standing, Landlord, Hamlet, and Special
                victory conditions.

    COMBAT

        In each combat round:

            1. The attacker's participating entities each take one action.
            2. The defender's participating entities each take one action.
            3. If neither side is defeated or gone, start another round.

        To attack, roll 1d6. If the roll is equal to or below the
        attacker's Attack, the attack hits and deals 1 damage unless a
        card says otherwise.

    FORTRESSES

        Only battlefield entities can capture enemy fortresses.

        If a fortress is assaulted, attackers may target the fortress or
        the entities inside. Defenders inside the fortress may attack the
        assaulting entities.

        If all defenders are gone and the fortress still has at least
        1 HP, the attacker may capture it by moving at least one
        surviving attacker inside.

        If the fortress reaches 0 HP, it is destroyed along with all
        entities and items inside it.

    POSITIONING

        Entities on the battlefield are exposed, but they threaten enemy
        fortresses. Entities inside fortresses are protected, but they
        give up capture pressure. Winning Goosklerf requires balancing
        protection and projection.

================================================================================
                              HOW TO READ THIS BOOK
================================================================================

    Goosklerf II is a modular card-and-dice game. The same deck can be used
    to play several different game modes, from quick skirmishes to full
    narrative campaigns.

    Goosklerf II can be played in three rules levels:

        BASIC GOOSKLERF uses only the Core Rules.

        ADVANCED GOOSKLERF uses the Core Rules plus all Advanced Rules.

        CUSTOM GOOSKLERF uses the Core Rules plus any Advanced Rules
        the players agree to before setup.

    Game modes may specify their own rules level.

    A player learning the game should read Quickstart, Parts I and II,
    skim the mode selector in Part V, and pick Classic Mode for their
    first game. Everything else can be ignored until the first game has
    been played.

                         PART I - COMPONENTS AND TABLE

1. WHAT YOU NEED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    To play Goosklerf II, players need:

        -   A deck of Goosklerf cards (their own, or a shared pool)
        -   At least one six-sided die per player
        -   Additional dice for tracking hit points (recommended: 1 extra
            die per entity in play)
        -   A flat playing surface large enough for the table layout
            (see Section 4)

    Optional but helpful:

        -   A printed play mat for the chosen game mode
        -   Small dice for tracking buffs, rounds, and fortress ownership
        -   A chessboard (only required for Chessklerf mode)
        -   Pen and paper (optional, as an alternative method for stat/HP tracking)
        -   Graph paper (optional, for the Campaign mode map)
        -   Character sheets (Campaign Mode)


2. CARD TYPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    There are three card types in Goosklerf II: entities, items, and
    fortresses. Each type has a normal and a rare variant, identified by
    border color.

    ENTITIES (normal: red border / rare: purple border)

        Entities are the fighters. They attack, equip items, occupy
        fortresses, and use special abilities.

        -   Only entities can equip and use items.
        -   Entities may attack enemy entities and enemy fortresses.
        -   An entity is defeated when its hit points reach 0.
        -   Some entities have Special text on the card. Special text
            always overrides the standard rules.

        A normal entity has one HP die. A Super Entity is a rare entity
        with two HP dice. Super Entities are marked clearly on the card.

    FORTRESSES (normal: blue border / rare: orange border)

        Fortresses are structures placed in the suburbs. They house
        entities, grant buffs or debuffs to the entities inside, and
        sometimes have special effects.

        -   Fortresses can house up to 3 entities.
        -   Fortresses cannot hold or use items directly.
        -   A fortress is destroyed when its hit points reach 0.
        -   When a fortress is destroyed, all entities inside it are
            destroyed, along with their equipped items.

        A normal fortress has two HP dice. A Super Fortress is a rare
        fortress with three HP dice. Super Fortresses are marked clearly
        on the card.

    ITEMS (normal: green border / rare: yellow or gold border)

        Items are equipment carried by entities. There are two subtypes:

        Equipped Items stay attached to an entity and provide a continuous
        effect (an attack buff, an HP buff, a special ability, etc.) until
        the item is discarded, transferred, or the entity dies.

        Consumables are used for a one-time effect, then discarded. A
        consumable must be equipped by an entity before it can be used.

        An entity can carry up to 3 items at a time (equipped items and
        consumables combined).

3. CARD ANATOMY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Every card has:

        A NAME at the top.

        A CARD-TYPE ICON indicating whether it is an entity, fortress,
        item, or consumable.

        A BORDER COLOR indicating rarity.

        STAT VALUES. Entities and fortresses have an Attack value and an
        HP value. Items may have buffs or debuffs to those values.

        A TEXT BOX containing any Special rules or flavor text. Special
        rules override general rules if there is a conflict.

    HOW STATS WORK

        ATTACK is the target number a player must roll at or below to
        land a normal hit. A base Attack of 3 means the entity hits on a
        roll of 1, 2, or 3 on a six-sided die. Attack can be modified by
        items, fortress buffs, and specials.

        HIT POINTS (HP) is tracked physically with a die placed on top
        of the card. When the card is played, place a die showing its
        maximum HP. Each time it takes damage, rotate the die down by
        the amount of damage taken. When the die reaches 0, the card is
        defeated.

        Normal entities use one HP die. Super entities use two HP dice.
        Normal fortresses use two HP dice. Super fortresses use three.
        Damage is applied one HP at a time across the dice, in any order
        the controlling player chooses.



4. THE TABLE: ZONES AND LAYOUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The play area is divided into zones. Players should know these terms
    because the rules refer to them constantly.

    THE BATTLEFIELD is the center of the table. It is a shared, open space
    where entities fight. All players use one battlefield. Players can hold
    up to 5 of their own entities in the battlefield.

    THE SUBURBS are the fortress zones along the edges of the table. Each
    player has their own suburbs. Fortresses are placed only in the
    suburbs. If the battlefield is the sea, the suburbs are the shoreline,
    and fortresses are ships moored along it.

    GRAVEYARD. Each player has their own graveyard (discard pile).
    Destroyed, defeated, spent, and discarded cards go here unless a rule
    says otherwise.

    DECK. Each player has their own deck. Players keep their deck face
    down nearby. Cards in the deck must be transferred to the HAND before
    they may be placed.

    HAND. Each player has a private hand of cards. This is a queue of
    cards that can be placed during the Card Play phase.

    SHOP (optional, used with the Shop rule). A face-up row of cards that
    can be purchased during the Card Play phase.

                              PART II - CORE RULES

5. CORE CONCEPTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Before the detailed rules, here are the concepts the rest of the book
    assumes the players understand.

    CONTROL. A card is controlled by the player who owns it, unless a
    rule or special effect changes control (for example, capturing a
    fortress).

    OCCUPY. A player occupies a fortress if they have at least one
    surviving entity inside it. A player can control an empty fortress.

    ENGAGEMENT. A single declared combat, from the moment combat is
    declared until one side is destroyed, retreats, or the engagement
    ends by rule. Engagements are resolved one at a time.

    CARD TEXT OVERRIDES. If a card's special text contradicts a general
    rule, the card text wins. If two cards contradict each other, the
    card belonging to the acting player wins.

    FORTRESS BUFFS. Fortresses modify the stats of entities inside
    them, the same way items modify the stats of entities carrying
    them. A fortress's buff or debuff (printed in its text box) applies
    to every entity occupying that fortress for as long as the entity
    remains inside. Buffs are lost immediately when the entity leaves
    the fortress during Combat Staging, Movement, Retreat, or any other
    movement. Fortress buffs apply during Initial Volley, Fortress
    Barrage, fortress assault defense, and all other combat actions
    taken while the entity is inside the fortress. Fortress buffs
    stack with item buffs, subject to the stat limits in Section 12.

6. ENTITY ACTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    During combat, each participating entity normally takes ONE action
    per combat round. The available actions are listed below.

    Some actions are always available. Others require Advanced Rules
    to be active.

    BASIC ACTIONS

        NORMAL ATTACK
            Choose one legal enemy target in the engagement and roll
            1d6. If the roll is equal to or below the acting entity's
            Attack value, the attack hits and deals 1 HP damage unless
            a card says otherwise.

        USE A CONSUMABLE
            Use one equipped consumable item. Resolve its effect, then
            discard it unless the card says otherwise.

        USE A SPECIAL ABILITY
            Use a special ability printed on the entity, item, fortress,
            or scenario, if its conditions are met.

    ADVANCED ACTIONS

        PARRY
            Attempt to cancel 1 HP of normal-hit damage suffered during
            the previous enemy round. Roll against the entity's Attack.
            On success, regain 1 HP lost from that hit.

        RETREAT
            Give up the entity's action and attempt to leave the
            engagement. If the entity survives the next enemy round and
            has a legal destination, it leaves the engagement.

        ITEM SWAP
            Transfer up to 2 equipped items from the acting entity to
            another friendly entity in the same engagement, respecting
            item capacity.

        HEAL
            Roll against the acting entity's Attack. On success, discard
            1 random card from the acting player's deck and heal one
            friendly entity in the engagement by +1 HP.

        SUPERCHARGED ATTACK
            Roll 3d6 instead of 1d6. If all three dice are equal to or
            below the acting entity's Attack value, deal 2 damage. If
            any die misses, the attack fails and the acting entity takes
            1 self-damage.

        BUY AN EXTRA ACTION
            Discard 3 random cards from the acting player's deck to give
            this entity one additional action this combat round. Extra
            Actions may be bought repeatedly if the player has enough
            cards in deck.

    REACTIONS AND SPECIAL COMBAT EVENTS

        LAST STAND
            When an entity is reduced to 0 HP, it may take one final
            action before being discarded. If that action raises its HP
            above 0, the entity survives.

        SCAVENGER
            When a friendly entity dies during an engagement, surviving
            friendly entities in that same engagement may immediately
            equip that dead ally's items if they have capacity.

        REINFORCEMENTS
            At the end of a combat round, a side may call one
            reinforcement into the engagement if Reinforcements are
            active.

        INITIAL VOLLEY
            When a real battlefield engagement begins, each eligible
            occupied fortress may fire one normal attack into that
            engagement before the first combat round begins. Initial
            Volley cannot be used unless at least one attacking entity
            has been committed to that battlefield engagement.

        FORTRESS BARRAGE
            Occupied fortresses may attack enemy fortresses directly
            if Fortress Barrage is active. Barrage cannot capture
            fortresses.

7. VICTORY CONDITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Goosklerf II uses four end conditions in multiplayer. The first one
    to trigger ends the game.

    LAST MAN STANDING. Only one player (or team) has any surviving
    entities in play. That player wins immediately.

    LANDLORD. A player becomes Landlord when they control every
    fortress in play AND no unplayed fortress cards remain in any
    player's deck or hand. This is not an instant win: the Landlord
    must survive one full round (starting from the next player's turn)
    still meeting both conditions to win.

    Landlord status is broken if, during the survival round:
        -   Another player captures one of the Landlord's fortresses.
        -   Another player plays a new fortress into their suburbs.
        -   A scenario effect introduces a new fortress.

    Destruction of a Landlord's fortress does NOT break status by
    itself, but if it leaves zero fortresses in play, status ends.
    A broken Landlord status can be re-achieved later; the survival
    round restarts from scratch.

    HAMLET. All entities are destroyed, and no player can legally put
    a new entity into play. Everyone loses.

    SPECIAL. A special effect on an item, entity, fortress, or scenario
    may create an additional victory condition. If that special effect
    is successfully resolved, its victory condition overrides the
    standard multiplayer victory conditions and the controlling player
    wins immediately.



8. DECK AND STARTING HAND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    DECK REGIMES. Goosklerf II uses three standardized deck sizes.
    Players must pick one before the game begins. All players must use
    the same number of cards for their individual decks.

        SMALL DECK (24 cards)
            Minimum card quantities:
                -   5 entities
                -   1 fortress
                -   10 items

            Shop size: 3 cards, drawn from the deck during setup.
            Recommended for: quick Classic games, Lightning Tactical
            practice, or playing with small collections.

        MEDIUM DECK (36 cards)
            Minimum card quantities:
                -   10 entities
                -   2 fortresses
                -   15 items
            Shop size: 7 cards, drawn from the deck during setup.
            Recommended for: default Classic Mode. This is the
            official standard deck size.

        LARGE DECK (54 cards)
            Minimum card quantities:
                -   15 entities
                -   3 fortresses
                -   25 items
            Shop size: 7 cards, drawn from the deck during setup.
            Recommended for: long Classic Mode sessions, team games,
            Campaign Mode setups, or large battlepaths.

    Decks may contain more entities and fortresses than the minimums
    listed (at the cost of fewer items). Decks may not go below the
    minimums listed. Any remaining deck slots may be filled with
    entities, fortresses, or items, as long as all minimums are met
    and the final deck size matches the selected regime exactly.

    A deck may include at most 3 copies of any card with the same name.

    STARTING HAND. At the start of the game, each player draws 7
    cards at random from their own deck. This is the only fully
    random draw in the game; cards drawn on subsequent turns involve
    a choice (see Section 9, Phase 4: Card Draw).

    MULLIGAN RULE. If a player's starting hand contains no entities or
    fortresses (no legal opening placement), they may reshuffle their
    deck and draw a new random hand. Other players keep their hands
    unchanged.

    FIRST PLAYER. Determine the first player by die roll, thumb war,
    arm wrestling, a game of speed chess, a round of a non-lethal combat
    sport, a pushup competition, or agreement. Play proceeds clockwise.
    A round ends when every surviving player has taken one turn.

    SHUFFLING. Every time a player looks through a deck, the deck must
    be thoroughly shuffled. In competitive games, the player to each
    player's left must shuffle the deck thoroughly at least 3 times
    before returning it to the original player. Players may not look
    through each other's decks unless explicitly called out on a card.




9. TURN STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    A turn consists of five phases, taken in order.

        1. CARD PLAY
        2. COMBAT
        3. MOVEMENT
        4. CARD DRAW
        5. VICTORY CHECK

    Each phase must be completed before moving to the next.

    PHASE 1: CARD PLAY

        The active player must play exactly 3 cards from their hand if
        possible. If it is impossible to play 3 cards, the maximum
        number of playable cards must be played.

        A card is played when it is:
            -   a fortress placed into the player's own suburbs
            -   an entity placed onto the battlefield
            -   an entity placed into one of the player's own fortresses
            -   an item equipped onto one of the player's entities
            -   a shop purchase, if the Shop rule is active

        If the active player cannot play 3 legal cards from their hand,
        they must discard cards from their hand until cards played plus
        cards discarded equals 3. (Example: only 2 cards can be played,
        so 1 must be discarded.)

        If the active player has fewer than 3 cards in hand at the
        start of Card Play, they play as many as they legally can and
        discard the rest. If their hand is empty, skip Card Play and
        proceed directly to Combat.

        Capacity limits still apply: 3 items per entity, 3 entities
        per fortress, 5 entities on the battlefield per player.

    PHASE 2: COMBAT

        The active player may declare any number of combat engagements
        this turn. Each engagement is resolved fully before the next
        begins. Each of the active player's entities may participate in
        only one engagement per turn, unless a special ability says
        otherwise.

        Combat is broken into three sub-phases:

        1. Combat Staging/Movement:
           The active player declares each engagement one at a time.
           For each engagement, the active player chooses which of their
           eligible entities are participating and moves them into the
           engagement area if required.

           Combat Staging is not general movement. It only moves entities
           into the engagement they are about to fight in. Entities moved
           during Combat Staging must immediately participate in that
           engagement.

        2. Combat Round(s):
           The participating attackers and defenders fight until one side
           is defeated, retreats, or the engagement ends by rule.

        3. Combat Resolution:
           Resolve capture, destruction, retreat destinations, Scavenger,
           and final positions for all surviving cards.

        See Section 10 for combat rules.

    PHASE 3: MOVEMENT

        The active player may freely reposition their own entities and
        transfer their own items during this phase, as long as all
        resulting positions are legal.

        Legal movement:
            -   Battlefield to one of the player's fortresses (with space)
            -   The player's fortress to the battlefield (if legal
                battlefield room exists)
            -   The player's fortress to another of the player's
                fortresses (with space)

        POSITIONING MATTERS

            End-of-Movement positions decide what a player is ready to
            do on their next turn.

            Only battlefield entities can assault and capture enemy
            fortresses. Entities inside fortresses can defend, fire
            Initial Volleys when legal, and use Fortress Barrage if that
            rule is active, but they cannot capture enemy fortresses.

            An undefended fortress has no entity defense. If it is
            assaulted by an enemy battlefield entity and survives the
            assault, the attacker may capture it immediately during
            Combat Resolution by moving at least one surviving attacker
            inside.

            Entities on the battlefield are exposed, but they threaten
            enemy fortresses. Entities inside fortresses are safer, but
            they give up capture pressure. Balance protection and
            projection.

    PHASE 4: CARD DRAW

        The active player draws the top 2 cards of their deck at random,
        then searches their deck for 1 card of their choice. Add all 3
        cards to hand.

        If fewer than 3 cards remain available, add as many as possible:
        draw random cards first, then search only if at least 1 card
        remains in the deck.

        After searching, reshuffle per Section 8.

    PHASE 5: VICTORY CHECK

        At the end of the turn, after Card Draw is complete, check
        whether any victory condition has been met (Section 7). If so,
        the game ends. Landlord status is checked and tracked here (see
        Section 7 for the survival-round rule). During the victory
        check, players must communicate whether or not they have any
        unplayed fortresses in their hand or deck, and may look
        through their own deck to confirm. Reshuffle per Section 8.



10. COMBAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Combat begins when the active player declares an engagement. The
    player declaring combat is the attacker. All opposing entities
    involved are the defenders.

    WHO CAN ATTACK WHOM

        Entities on the battlefield may attack:
            -   Enemy battlefield entities
            -   Enemy fortresses in the suburbs (fortress assault)

        Entities inside a fortress may attack only in these cases:
            -   They leave the fortress during Combat Staging and join
                a battlefield engagement.
            -   They fire one Initial Volley into a battlefield
                engagement, if legal.
            -   They defend their own fortress during a fortress assault.
            -   They attack an enemy fortress through Fortress Barrage,
                if that rule is active.

        Entities inside a fortress cannot make normal attacks against
        battlefield entities while remaining inside the fortress.

    INITIAL VOLLEY

        When a battlefield engagement is declared, each eligible occupied
        fortress may fire one Initial Volley before the first combat
        round begins.

        A battlefield engagement must include at least one attacking
        entity committed to the engagement. A player cannot declare an
        empty engagement just to trigger Initial Volley.

        A fortress may fire an Initial Volley only if:
            -   it has at least one surviving occupant,
            -   it is not currently under assault,
            -   it is not participating in Fortress Barrage, and
            -   the engagement is a battlefield engagement, not a
                fortress assault.

        Each fortress may fire at most one Initial Volley per engagement,
        regardless of how many entities are inside it. The controlling
        player chooses which occupant fires.

        The volley is one normal attack against one enemy entity
        participating in the battlefield engagement. Volleys cannot target fortresses.

    COMBAT ROUNDS

        Combat proceeds in alternating rounds. In each round:

            1. The attacker's participating entities each take ONE action.
            2. The defender's participating entities each take ONE action.
            3. If one side has been fully destroyed, the engagement ends.
               Otherwise, begin the next round.

        Actions available to each entity on its turn:

            A. Normal Attack
            B. Use a Consumable
            C. Use a Special Ability

        If playing Advanced or Custom Goosklerf, additional actions may
        include:

            D. Parry
            E. Retreat
            F. Item Swap
            G. Heal
            H. Supercharged Attack
            I. Buy an Extra Action

    10.1 COMBAT STAGING/MOVEMENT

        During Combat Staging, the active player declares one engagement
        at a time. The terms below describe the engagement's SCOPE
        (which enemy cards are eligible), not simultaneous attacks —
        individual attacks still target one legal enemy at a time unless
        a card says otherwise.

        Battlefield entities may be staged into:
            1. A battlefield engagement targeting one enemy player. All
               of that player's battlefield entities are eligible
               defenders in the engagement.
            2. A fortress assault targeting one or more of that enemy
               player's fortresses.

        Entities inside a fortress may participate in combat by:
            1. Leaving the fortress during Combat Staging to join a
               battlefield engagement.
            2. Firing an Initial Volley into a battlefield engagement,
               if legal.
            3. Defending their own fortress during a fortress assault.
            4. Participating in Fortress Barrage, if that rule is active.

        Entities that leave a fortress during Combat Staging are now
        battlefield entities for that engagement. They are exposed,
        targetable, and may not return to a fortress until a later legal
        movement, retreat, capture, or combat-resolution rule allows it.

        Entities inside a fortress cannot capture an enemy fortress.
        Only battlefield entities may capture fortresses.

    10.2 NORMAL ATTACK

        1. Declare a target. The attacker chooses one legal enemy target
           in this engagement (one entity, or the fortress if this is a
           fortress assault).

        2. Roll one 6-sided die.

        3. If the result is equal to or below the acting entity's current
           modified Attack value, the attack hits. Otherwise it misses.

        4. A normal hit deals 1 HP of damage unless a special rule or
           card text changes the amount. Reduce the target's HP by the
           HP damage upon a successful attack.

    10.3 FORTRESS ASSAULT

        In a fortress assault, attacking battlefield entities may target
        either the fortress or any defending entity inside it. Defending
        entities inside the fortress may target attackers normally.
        Attackers may choose to target one or more enemy fortresses in
        the same engagement. Multi-fortress engagements are legal: There
        is no cap on the number of fortresses targeted in a single
        engagement. Each targeted fortress resolves capture or
        destruction independently.

        A fortress assault ends when:
            -   All defending entities are killed or retreat, OR
            -   All attackers are killed or retreat, OR
            -   The fortress is destroyed.

        CAPTURING A FORTRESS. If all defending entities of a fortress
        are killed or retreat and the fortress still has at least 1 HP,
        the surviving attackers may choose to capture or destroy the
        fortress.

            CAPTURE: Control of the fortress transfers to the attacker.
            Up to the fortress's capacity in attackers may move inside
            it; any excess attackers stay on the battlefield. At least
            1 entity must be garrisoned in the fortress upon first
            capture to keep the fortress. If no entities are moved into
            a newly captured fortress, then the fortress remains under
            its previous owner's control. A victorious attacker may
            decide how to split surviving entities between the
            battlefield and/or the captured fortress. A victorious
            attacker does not need to capture a fortress and may leave
            it empty but still under enemy control.

            DESTROY: Discard the fortress. No one controls it. Attackers
            remain on the battlefield.

        DESTROYING A FORTRESS. If the fortress itself is reduced to 0 HP
        during the assault, it is destroyed. All entities inside it, and
        their equipped items, are immediately destroyed with it. (If the
        Last Stand rule is active, defenders may take their final action
        before discarding.) If defenders retreat before the fortress is
        destroyed, they can survive. If defenders did not choose to
        retreat before the fortress's HP reached 0, all defenders die.



11. DAMAGE, DEFEAT, AND ITEM OWNERSHIP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    11.1 APPLYING DAMAGE

        When an entity or fortress takes damage, rotate its HP die down
        by the amount of damage taken. Healing works in reverse but can
        never raise an HP die above its printed maximum unless a card
        explicitly says so.

    11.2 HP DOES NOT RESET

        Damage persists between combats. An entity that survives one
        engagement with 2 HP remaining enters its next engagement still
        at 2 HP. The only way to heal is through an item, consumable,
        Healing action (Section 18), or special ability that explicitly
        restores HP.

        This rule may be disabled for casual play.

    11.3 DEFEAT

        An entity is defeated when its HP reaches 0. The entity and all
        of its equipped items are placed in its owner's graveyard.

        A fortress is destroyed when its HP reaches 0. The fortress is
        placed in its owner's graveyard, along with every entity inside
        it and every item those entities were carrying.

    11.4 ITEM OWNERSHIP

        Each player has exclusive control of their own cards. There is
        no transfer of cards between players. When a card is discarded,
        it returns to the graveyard of its original owner. This prevents
        item-theft ambiguity.



12. STAT LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Unless a card explicitly overrides them:

            STAT              MINIMUM     MAXIMUM
            Attack               1           5
            HP (per die)         0           6
            Items per entity     0           3
            Entities per fort    0           3
            Battlefield entities 0           5 per player

    If a buff would push a value above its cap, it stops at the cap. If
    a debuff would push a value below its minimum, it stops at the
    minimum (except HP, which can reach 0 and defeat the card).

    If a change in capacity would leave an entity or fortress holding
    more than the new maximum, the controlling player immediately
    chooses which cards remain legal and discards the excess to their
    graveyard.



================================================================================
                             PART III - ADVANCED RULES
================================================================================

    Advanced Rules expand the Core Rules.

    In Basic Goosklerf, ignore this part.

    In Advanced Goosklerf, use every rule in this part.

    In Custom Goosklerf, players choose which Advanced Rules are active
    before setup.

    A rules-selection sheet is provided in the appendix for Custom
    Goosklerf.



A. ECONOMY AND GAME PRESSURE
================================================================================

13. SHOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    At the start of the game, each player draws cards at random from
    their deck and places them face-up in their own shop area. The
    number of shop cards depends on the deck regime (see Section 8):

            Small Deck:    3 shop cards
            Medium Deck:   7 shop cards
            Large Deck:    7 shop cards

    Shop cards belong to the player who placed them. Only that player
    may buy from their own shop. All shop cards are always visible to
    all players throughout the game.

    BUYING. During the active player's Card Play phase, they may
    purchase one or more shop cards. To buy 1 shop card, the active
    player must do one of the following:

        -   Discard 2 normal cards from their hand, OR
        -   Discard 1 rare card from their hand (rare = purple entity,
            orange fortress, or yellow/gold item).

    A purchased card must be played or equipped immediately using the
    normal placement rules. If it cannot be legally placed, it cannot
    be purchased. Each shop purchase counts as one of the active
    player's 3 played cards for the turn.

    The shop does not refill on its own. Once a shop card is bought,
    that slot is gone for the rest of the game unless a special rule
    refills it.



14. OLD AGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    At the end of a player's turn, if that player has gone 3 of their
    own turns without declaring an engagement, randomly choose one of
    their surviving entities. That entity dies and is discarded.

    Declaring any engagement resets the count. Initial Volley does not
    reset the count. Fortress Barrage does.

    Old Age cannot be prevented, healed, revived, or used for Last Stand.

    If a player's last entity dies from Old Age, that player loses.



B. COMBAT ACTIONS
================================================================================

15. PARRY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Instead of taking a normal action during its turn in combat, an
    entity may declare a Parry.

    The parrying entity chooses one normal hit that was landed on it
    during the previous enemy round. Roll 1d6. If the roll is equal to
    or below the parrying entity's Attack stat, the parry succeeds: The
    entity regains 1 HP lost from that hit.

    Parry only cancels 1 HP of damage from a single normal attack. It
    cannot cancel damage from specials, consumables, or extra damage
    bonuses from other rules.

    A parry attempt consumes the entity's action for that round,
    whether it succeeds or fails. An entity can only parry one hit at
    a time.



16. RETREAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Instead of attacking on its turn in combat, an entity may declare
    a retreat. The entity announces its intent to retreat and takes no
    action this round.

    During the next round, enemy entities in the engagement may target
    the retreating entity normally. If the retreating entity survives
    that round, it leaves the engagement at the end of the round.

    A retreating entity must have a legal destination:
        -   Battlefield entities may retreat into a friendly fortress
            that has room.
        -   Fortress entities under assault may retreat into the
            battlefield if battlefield room exists, or into another
            friendly fortress if one has room.

    If no legal destination exists, the entity cannot retreat.




17. ITEM SWAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Instead of taking any other action in combat, an entity may transfer
    up to two of its equipped items to another friendly entity in the
    same engagement. The acting entity does not attack this round. The
    receiving entity must have room for the incoming items.

    Items cannot be transferred to enemies. Consumables that have
    already been activated cannot be transferred.



18. HEALING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Entities have the choice to heal themselves or another friendly
    entity in the same engagement. To heal, the acting entity rolls for
    an attack (1d6 against its modified Attack stat). On a hit, the
    healer randomly draws a card from their own deck and discards it.
    This heals any single friendly entity in the engagement by +1 HP.
    On a miss, the entity's action is consumed and no card is discarded.

    Healing cannot raise an entity's HP above its printed maximum unless
    a card explicitly says so.



19. SUPERCHARGED ATTACK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Instead of a normal attack, an entity may declare a Supercharged
    Attack. Roll 3d6 instead of 1d6.

        -   If all three dice are equal to or below the entity's Attack
            value, the attack deals 2 damage instead of 1.
        -   If any one die misses, the attack fails and the attacking
            entity takes 1 self-damage.

    A Supercharged Attack consumes the entity's action for the round.



20. EXTRA ACTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In combat, entities use only ONE action per round by default. An
    entity may "buy" one additional action by drawing from its stamina
    reserves: randomly draw 3 cards from the entity's controlling
    player's deck and discard them. This grants the entity one
    additional action this round, which may be any normal action.

    The cost stacks (two extra actions = 6 discarded cards), with no
    cap other than deck depth.





C. COMBAT EVENTS
================================================================================

21. LAST STAND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    When an entity is reduced to 0 HP, it may take one final action
    before being discarded: a normal attack, a consumable, a special
    ability, or a Heal (if the Healing rule is active).

    Last Stand triggers from any source of 0 HP, including self-damage
    from Supercharged Attack (Section 19).

    After taking its final action, the entity is placed in its owner's
    graveyard along with its items. An entity killed by Last Stand is
    defeated normally. If the Last Stand action causes the entity's HP
    to increase to 1 or more (e.g., via a successful Heal), the entity
    remains alive and is not discarded.

    An entity may take only one Last Stand action each time it is
    reduced to 0 HP. If it survives because that action restored HP, it
    does not receive another Last Stand from the same damage event.




22. REINFORCEMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    At the end of either side's combat round, that side may call ONE
    reinforcement into the engagement.

    To call a reinforcement, choose one friendly entity already in the
    engagement. That entity becomes the caller. The caller's NEXT
    action is spent calling the reinforcement instead of attacking,
    healing, retreating, or using another action. If the caller is
    defeated before its next action, the reinforcement still arrives,
    but the caller is defeated normally.

    Only one reinforcement per side per round may be called, but there
    is no cap on total reinforcements over the course of the
    engagement.

    Defenders inside a fortress under assault are isolated: They cannot
    CALL or RECEIVE reinforcements during that engagement. Their
    fortress is surrounded.

    The reinforcing entity must come from a legal nearby zone:
        -   One of the calling player's own fortresses in the suburbs
        -   The battlefield

    A reinforcement enters the engagement immediately and can be
    targeted by enemies in the next round. It cannot act during the
    round it arrives. After the engagement ends, the reinforcement
    remains wherever it reinforced into: battlefield reinforcements
    stay on the battlefield, fortress reinforcements stay in that
    fortress.

    A reinforcement may not be called into a location that is already
    at its cap: No reinforcement into a fortress that already holds 3
    entities, no reinforcement onto a battlefield already holding 5 of
    that player's entities.

    Reinforcements may be called into Fortress Barrage engagements
    (Section 24), but defenders inside a battlefield-assaulted fortress
    are still isolated as described above.



23. SCAVENGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    If a friendly entity is defeated during an engagement, allied
    entities (controlled by the SAME player) in that same engagement
    may immediately equip the dead entity's items if they have capacity.
    Any items that cannot be equipped are discarded normally.

    Scavenger applies only to a player's OWN dead allies' items. A
    player may never pick up an enemy's items. Each player retains
    exclusive control of their own cards.

    Items that were already equipped by other entities cannot be
    transferred to a dead entity to "save" them for scavenging. This
    rule only applies at the moment of defeat.




D. FORTRESS COMBAT
================================================================================

24. FORTRESS BARRAGE (FORTRESS-VS-FORTRESS COMBAT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Fortresses may be thought of like ships fighting across open water.
    A fortress with occupants may engage an enemy fortress directly,
    without moving through the battlefield.

    To declare a Fortress Barrage:
        -   The attacking fortress must have at least one entity inside.
        -   The target is a single enemy fortress.
        -   Entities inside the attacking fortress may target only the
            target fortress, not its occupants.
        -   Defending entities inside the target fortress may target
            attacking entities inside the attacking fortress normally
            (they fire back).
        -   Combat resolves in alternating rounds like a standard
            battlefield engagement.

    A fortress cannot be captured through a Fortress Barrage. Only
    battlefield assaults can capture a fortress. If the Barrage kills
    all of the target fortress's occupants without destroying the
    fortress itself, the fortress stands empty under its previous
    controller's ownership.

    If the target fortress is destroyed, all its occupants and their
    items are destroyed with it.


================================================================================
                PART IV - BACKWARDS COMPATIBILITY (GOOSKLERF I)
================================================================================


    Goosklerf II simplified the stat system from Goosklerf I (GKI).
    GKI used Attack, Defense, and Capacity stats. GKII uses only Attack
    and HP, with a fixed capacity of 3. When a player uses a mixed
    deck, the following conversions apply.

    DEFENSE CONVERSION. When a GKI card references Defense in GKII
    play, treat the Defense value as Attack when that entity is
    defending (not the initiator of combat). If a GKI fortress buffs
    Defense on a GKII entity, apply the buff to that entity's Attack
    stat instead.

    CAPACITY CONVERSION. GKI cards retain their printed capacity
    values. GKII cards use a fixed capacity of 3 items per entity and
    3 entities per fortress, regardless of any GKI capacity effects
    that might target them.

        -   If a GKI effect would raise a GKII entity's capacity, the
            GKII entity's capacity stays at 3.
        -   If a GKI effect would raise another GKI card's capacity,
            apply it normally using GKI rules.
        -   Overflow from mid-battle capacity changes is handled as in
            Section 12: the controlling player picks which cards to
            discard.

    MIXED ITEM BUFFS. If a GKI item buffs both Attack and Defense, use
    the relevant value based on whether the holding entity is attacking
    or defending during that combat round.

    LEGACY OFF. Players may also simply ignore Defense and Capacity on
    GKI cards and play them as pure GKII cards. This is allowed for
    casual play.



================================================================================
                             PART V - GAME MODES
================================================================================



25. MODE SELECTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Goosklerf II has five official modes.

    CLASSIC MODE. The flagship multiplayer experience. Full rules, full
    decks, full war. 2 to 4 players.

    TACTICAL MODE. A smaller, faster, more structured battle with no
    hand refill and fixed starting loadouts. 2 to 4 players. Three
    deck-size variants: Lightning (tiny), Standard Tactical (medium),
    and Skirmish (overpowered).

    SINGLEPLAYER MODE. A solo battlepath game. The player fights
    through a series of enemy fortresses. 1 player.

    CAMPAIGN MODE. A narrative, RPG-like mode with a Dungeon Master and
    scripted scenarios. 1 DM plus 1 to 4 players.

    CHESSKLERF. A positional variant played on a chessboard. 2 players.

    Each mode tells the players which core and advanced rules to use.



26. CLASSIC MODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Classic Mode is the standard way to play Goosklerf II. 2 to 4
    players. Last player standing, or first to Landlord, wins.

    SETUP

        All players agree on a deck regime before setup (see
        Section 8):

            -   SMALL (24 cards) for a fast game.
            -   MEDIUM (36 cards) is the default Classic experience.
            -   LARGE (54 cards) for long grinding sessions.

        Each player builds a legal deck of the chosen regime size,
        respecting the minimum composition rules in Section 8.

        SHARED-POOL DRAFT (competitive play). If players are building
        decks from a shared pool, shuffle the shared pool and place it
        face-down. Create a face-up draft row beside it.

        On each player's draft pick, they may either:
            -   draw the top card of the shared pool and add it to
                their deck, OR
            -   take one card from the face-up draft row and add it to
                their deck.

        If a player draws from the shared pool and does not want that
        card, they place it face-up into the draft row and their pick
        ends.

        Continue until each player has built a legal deck for the
        chosen deck regime.

        If the Shop rule is in effect, each player sets up their
        Shop now (3 cards for Small, 7 cards for Medium or Large).

        Then each player draws 7 random cards as their opening hand.

        No player begins with any of their cards on the table. Fortresses must
        be played from hand into the suburbs during Card Play.

        Randomly determine the first player with dice or use another
        means for casual play. Play proceeds clockwise.

    VICTORY

        Standard Victory Conditions (Section 7): Last Man Standing,
        Landlord (requires surviving one full round after claiming
        Landlord status), Hamlet (loss for all), or Special.



27. TACTICAL MODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Tactical Mode is a fixed-loadout battle with no hand refill. Every
    card a player gets is what they start with. 2 to 4 players.

    Tactical Mode comes in three deck-size variants. Players must
    choose one before starting.

    27.1 LIGHTNING (FAST)

        Each player gets:
            -   3 entities
            -   1 fortress
            -   7 items (distributed as desired, up to 1 per entity to
                start)

        No deck, no hand. At setup, each player places their cards as
        desired on the battlespace. The fortress goes in the player's
        suburbs and entities are placed either on the battlefield or in
        the fortress.

        Each round, players take turns performing Combat and Movement
        phases only. No Card Play, no Card Draw, and no refill.

        Lightning uses Basic Goosklerf by default. Players may use Custom
        Goosklerf by adding Retreat and Parry. Adanced Goosklerf optional.

    27.2 STANDARD TACTICAL

        Each player gets:
            -   5 entities
            -   1 fortress
            -   11 items selected before the game begins
            -   7-item reserve hand (the items not yet equipped)

        At setup, each player places their fortress in their suburbs
        and their entities on the battlefield or in their fortress.
        Each entity may begin with up to 2 equipped items.

        Turn structure is Combat, Movement, then an Equipment Phase
        where the active player may equip any items from their reserve
        hand to their entities, respecting capacity. No deck is used.

        Standard Tactical uses Advanced Goosklerf by default.


    27.3 SKIRMISH (OVERPOWERED)

        Each player gets:
            -   4 entities
            -   1 fortress
            -   12 items (any combination)

        This mode is a single, brutal battle. Whoever has surviving
        entities at the end wins. No Shop. No refill phase.

        Skirmish uses Advanced Goosklerf by default.

    VICTORY (ALL TACTICAL VARIANTS)

        Tactical Mode does not use Landlord, Hamlet, Card Draw, or
        deck-based victory conditions.

        A player wins Tactical Mode if:
            -   all enemy entities are defeated, OR
            -   all enemy fortresses are destroyed.

        If all players lose their final entity in the same combat
        round, the game is a draw.


    [ILLUSTRATION: Three small tactical layouts side by side, one for
    each variant. Each shows player slots, fortress, entity zone, and
    item reserve (where applicable). Small and clean, not detailed.]



28. SINGLEPLAYER MODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Singleplayer Mode is a solo battlepath game. You fight through a
    chain of enemy-held fortresses, keeping whatever items and entities
    you can salvage along the way.

    28.1 SETUP

        Sort your deck into three piles: fortresses, entities, items.
        Shuffle each pile separately and place all three face-down.

        Build the battlepath face-down by drawing fortresses and
        entities blindly and placing them at each level. The level
        compositions are:

            Level 1:  1 fortress, 1 entity, no items
            Level 2:  1 fortress, 1 entity with 1 item
            Level 3:  1 fortress, 2 entities with 1 item each
            Level 4:  1 fortress, 3 entities with 1 item each
            Level 5:  1 fortress, 3 entities with 2 items each
            Level 6:  1 fortress, 3 entities with 3 items each
            Level 7+: 2 fortresses with 3 entities per fortress, each
                      with 3 items (repeat as long as you have cards)

        Do not look at the face-down enemy cards as you place them.

        After building the battlepath, choose 3 entities from the
        remaining cards for your starting strike force. Draw 3 item
        cards at random and equip them to your strike force as you
        wish.

        Shuffle the remaining items and entities together to form your
        reserve deck. Reveal 7 cards from the top of that deck into
        your shop. The shop is always face-up and always visible.

    28.2 TURN FLOW

        Attack the next level on the battlepath. Flip the fortress
        and all entities inside face-up, and resolve the combat as
        a fortress assault.

        If you defeat the fortress and all its defenders, go to the
        Post-Battle phase below.

        If any of your entities die, remove them. If all of your
        entities die, the game ends in defeat.

    28.3 ENEMY AI

        Enemy entities behave like this, in order:

            1. If an enemy has an equipped consumable: think of a
               number from 1 to 6 in your head.
               Roll 1d6. If the die matches your number, the enemy
               uses the consumable on a legal target, and it is
               revealed.

            2. Otherwise the enemy performs a normal attack.

        ENEMY TARGETING. Enemies target your entity with the lowest
        current HP. In case of a tie, they target the entity with the
        highest Attack. In case of a further tie, choose randomly.

        ENEMY ITEMS ARE HIDDEN. Keep enemy equipped items face-down
        until the psychic roll triggers their use, then flip and resolve
        normally.

        BOSS FORTRESSES. Every third fortress on the battlepath
        (Level 3, 6, 9, etc.) is a Boss Fortress. At Levels 9 and
        beyond, BOTH fortresses on that level are Boss Fortresses.
        Each Boss Fortress's final surviving entity gains Last Stand
        automatically, even if you are not otherwise playing with
        Last Stand. Boss Last Stand follows the standard Last Stand
        rules in Section 21: revival via Healing is permitted.

        ENEMY REINFORCEMENTS. At the start of each enemy combat round
        at Level 4 or higher, roll 1d6. On a 6, one entity from the
        NEXT level on the battlepath is pulled forward and deployed
        into the current fortress as reinforcement (if the fortress
        has room). Flip this entity face-up and equip it with whatever
        items it was carrying. Remove it from its original level. If
        the next level is empty, no reinforcement is deployed.

    28.4 POST-BATTLE

        After you defeat a fortress:

            1. Add the fortress to your trophy pile.
            2. You may take any items carried by the defeated enemies,
               up to your strike force's item capacity. Discard the
               rest.
            3. You may make shop purchases. Spending costs:
                    -   2 normal cards = 1 shop card
                    -   1 rare card = 1 shop card
               Fortresses in your trophy pile may be spent as currency
               at the same rates. Spent trophy fortresses are removed
               from your trophy pile (and from your final score).
            4. The shop does not refill on its own.

        Purchased entities join your strike force. Purchased items are
        equipped immediately (or discarded if you have no room).

        If you purchased nothing, you cannot refill entities mid-run
        except by scavenging enemy items.

    28.5 SCORING

        Final score is calculated after the game ends, whether by
        victory or defeat.

            Each level cleared:                         100 points
            Each trophy fortress remaining unspent:      50 points
            Each surviving entity:                       25 points
            Each surviving equipped item:                10 points

        Optional rarity modifier for fortresses in the trophy pile:
            Super fortress:   x3
            Rare fortress:    x2
            Normal fortress:  x1



29. CAMPAIGN MODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Campaign Mode is the RPG mode of Goosklerf II. One player is the
    Dungeon Master. The other players (1 to 4) are Adventurers. The
    DM runs a scenario; the players play through it using their cards
    as character sheets.

    29.1 CHARACTERS

        Each player chooses one entity to serve as their main character.
        Optionally, players may add up to 2 additional entities to their
        battle party as sidekicks. The player's starting stats (Attack,
        HP) are the stats printed on the card.

        Each entity is also dealt 3 items as starting gear. Players
        draw 7 cards to form a personal reserve hand.

        Character progression comes from leveling (Section 29.5).

    29.2 THE DUNGEON MASTER

        The DM represents the world, the enemies, and the story. The
        DM's job is to:

            -   Describe each scene and let players react.
            -   Run all enemies in combat, rolling attacks and making
                target decisions.
            -   Adjudicate non-combat actions (see Section 29.3).
            -   Advance the story between encounters.
            -   Decide when characters level up.

        The DM uses cards from the shared deck as enemies, fortresses,
        rooms, loot, and set dressing. Pull a fortress card to represent
        a significant location. Pull entity cards to represent any
        hostile encounter. Pull item cards as loot.

    29.3 NON-COMBAT ACTIONS (CHECKS)

        When a character attempts something uncertain outside combat
        (picking a lock, persuading a stranger, sprinting across broken
        terrain, remembering something), the DM sets a difficulty:

            Easy       roll 5 or less on 1d6
            Normal     roll 4 or less on 1d6
            Hard       roll 3 or less on 1d6
            Desperate  roll 2 or less on 1d6
            Hail Mary  roll 1 on 1d6
            Impossible roll 1 on 2d6 (two 6-sided dice)

        The player rolls the die or dice required by the chosen
        difficulty. On success, the action works. On failure, the DM
        describes the consequence.

        Items and specials may modify the difficulty by +1 or -1 at
        the DM's discretion.

    29.4 COMBAT IN CAMPAIGN MODE

        Combat uses standard Goosklerf II attack rules (roll under
        Attack to hit). The DM controls all enemy turns.

        VARIABLE DAMAGE. Unlike standard Goosklerf II, a successful
        normal-attack hit in Campaign Mode deals variable damage to
        enemies:

            1-3 on 1d6:   1 damage (a scrape)
            4-5 on 1d6:   2 damage (a solid hit)
            6   on 1d6:   3 damage (a devastating blow)

        Variable damage applies ONLY to normal attacks. Card-printed
        damage from consumables, specials, or AOE effects ignores the
        variable damage table and uses the printed value (e.g., a
        consumable that says "deals 3 HP" still deals 3).

        PLAYER DAMAGE (plot armor). If a player is successfully hit by
        an enemy, the DM rolls to determine the variable damage done to
        the player. Campaign Mode is softer than standard play because
        the goal is completing the narrative, not attrition.

            1-2 on 1d6:   0 damage  (a graze, barely felt)
            3-4 on 1d6:   1 damage  (a real hit)
            5-6 on 1d6:   2 damage  (a serious blow)

        Roll the appropriate damage die after each successful normal
        attack: use the enemy-damage table when a player damages an
        enemy, and the player-damage table when an enemy damages a
        player. This makes fights swingier and more cinematic.

        The DM may override damage for dramatic effect: a boss might
        always deal a minimum of 2; a glancing shot might deal 0.

        Player HP persists between encounters, as in Classic Mode.
        Healing comes from items, consumables, specials, the Healing
        action (Section 18), or rest between acts (DM's call).

    29.5 LEVELING

        Characters level up at the end of each major act of the
        scenario, or whenever the DM decides the players have earned
        it.

            Level 2:  +1 maximum HP
            Level 3:  Once per session, reroll one failed roll
            Level 4:  +1 Attack (up to the maximum of 5)
            Level 5:  Squire Slot: a player may assign one extra entity
                      from their reserve as a squire. The squire holds
                      1 item and absorbs damage in the player's place:
                      when the player would take damage, they may
                      assign it to the squire first. If the squire dies,
                      the player loses it.
            Level 6:  +1 maximum HP
            Level 7:  +1 item capacity (4 instead of 3)
            Level 8+: Alternate between +1 HP, +1 reroll per session,
                      and one DM-approved custom perk.

    29.6 STRUCTURE OF A SCENARIO

        Every official Campaign scenario is structured in 4 acts:

            Act I:    Entrance. Set the scene, establish the goal,
                      introduce the weird local rule.
            Act II:   Complication. A new threat or moral decision
                      raises the stakes.
            Act III:  Collapse. Pressure builds. Resources tighten.
                      Enemies escalate.
            Act IV:   Finale. Confront the core problem. Win, lose, or
                      make a costly choice.

        Between acts, characters may level up and (at the DM's
        discretion) rest to restore HP.

    29.7 DEATH AND REVIVAL

        A character reduced to 0 HP is not dead unless the scenario
        says so. They are downed. At the end of the encounter, if
        their allies survive, the character recovers with 1 HP.

        If the character is downed during Act IV, or during a
        scripted "no second chances" beat, they die permanently.

        This is softer than standard Goosklerf II because Campaign
        Mode is about story, not attrition.

    29.8 HEALING

        Players may use a healing action via the advanced Healing rules
        (Section 18). Alternatively, the DM may lay out special healing
        zones on the map where players may restore some HP (such as in
        a hospital).

    29.9 SPECIAL ITEMS

        Players equip normal item cards but may also equip up to 3
        additional items invented by the DM with capabilities specified
        by the DM. These invented items must be recorded on the
        player's character sheet(s).

    29.10 CHARACTER SHEETS

        Full character stats are not limited by cards in Campaign Mode.
        Stats, items, level, and other elements are recorded on each
        entity's character sheet.

        A printable Campaign character sheet is provided in the
        appendix.


    29.11 MAP

        Before the campaign, the DM draws or selects a map on grid
        paper. The map shows where enemies, items, fortresses, and
        other special elements lie. The DM keeps a master copy with all
        elements marked (or tracks them by written coordinates), and
        reveals a player-facing version progressively, with fog of war
        optionally enabled by covering parts of the map. Entities
        cannot move through boundaries unless the DM allows it. When a
        character lands on a special square, an event or combat
        triggers, or the entity picks up an item.

        In combat, allies within a 5x5 square around the battle party
        may assist; allies farther away cannot.

        A campaign may be played without a map, using narrative only.

    29.12 MOVEMENT

        Each main character (with their party) occupies one grid space.
        Roll 1d6; the character may move up to that many spaces, capped
        at their maximum movement (default 3). Rolls above the cap
        count as the cap. Movement is optional — a character may move
        less than the roll allows, or not at all.



30. CHESSKLERF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Chessklerf is a positional variant for 2 players. It combines
    Goosklerf combat with chess movement. Players need a chessboard, 4
    chess pieces per player (queen, knight, rook, bishop) and the
    pawns from a standard chess set, plus Goosklerf cards.

    Chessklerf is not backwards-compatible with standard chess rules.
    Castling, en passant, and check are all ignored.

    30.1 SETUP

        Each player selects or draws 4 entities to be their "major
        pieces" and places one chess piece on top of each entity card
        to designate its role:

            King/Queen  (most flexible)
            Knight      (jumps)
            Rook        (orthogonal long-range)
            Bishop      (diagonal long-range)

        Place the corresponding chess piece on each entity (e.g., the
        King or Queen on the player's Queen/King entity, the knight on
        the player's knight entity, etc.) to denote which entity is
        which.

        Each major piece may be equipped with up to 2 items from the
        player's hand before the game begins.

        Place the 4 major pieces on the player's back rank. The two
        corner squares of each player's back rank correspond to that
        player's fortresses: any major piece sitting on a corner square
        is considered to occupy the fortress. The fortress grants
        standard fortress buffs to the entities garrisoned in the
        corners.

        Place the pawns in the middle of the board. Each player's
        pawns occupy their side of the middle two ranks (ranks 4 and
        5 of the standard 8x8 board, not ranks 2 and 7). This is the
        "opposite of regular chess" setup: pawns start facing each
        other directly across the center, major pieces at the back.

        SIDE ITEM PILE. All item cards not used as starting equipment
        for major pieces are set aside face-down at the start of the
        game as the side item pile. This is used for pawn capture
        rewards (Section 30.3).

        First player is decided by coin flip, agreement, or (safe)
        combat (GOOSNAV LLC not liable).

    30.2 TURNS

        On a player's turn, they may:
            -   Move one major piece according to its chess movement
                rules, OR
            -   Move one pawn forward one square

        Major pieces move exactly as they do in chess, with no limit
        on distance for queens, rooks, and bishops (blocked by other
        pieces normally), and knights jumping L-shapes.

    30.3 CAPTURING AND COMBAT

        PAWN CAPTURE. A pawn captures enemy pieces as in chess
        (diagonally forward one square). A pawn capture is resolved
        instantly with no combat: the captured piece is defeated and
        removed. The pawn stays on its new square. As a reward for
        the capture, the capturing player draws 1 item card from the
        side item pile and adds it to their hand.

        MAJOR PIECE CAPTURE. When a major piece moves onto a square
        occupied by an enemy piece, combat is initiated between the
        two pieces (not resolved instantly). Use standard Goosklerf
        II combat rules, attacker-first. The attacker is the piece
        that moved.

            IF THE ATTACKER WINS, the defending piece is removed and
            the attacker ends its turn on the captured square.

            IF THE DEFENDER WINS, the attacker is removed and the
            defender stays on its square.

            IF THE ATTACKER OR DEFENDER RETREATS successfully (using
            the standard Retreat rule), the retreating piece moves to
            any legal empty square it could have moved to instead of
            the capture square. A retreating piece may not capture
            another piece as part of retreat. If the defender
            retreats, the attacker remains in the defender's old
            position.

        FORTRESS CAPTURE. If a major piece enters an enemy corner
        fortress square, combat is initiated as above. If the
        attacker wins, they capture the fortress.

        CHESSKLERF FORTRESS VICTORY. If a player controls both enemy
        corner fortresses at the start of their turn, they win
        immediately.

    30.4 PAWN PROMOTION (EXPLOSION)

        If a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board (the enemy's
        back rank), it explodes. Remove the pawn and every piece or
        pawn in a 3x3 square centered on the exploding pawn's square.
        This includes friendly pieces. Fortresses are not destroyed
        by pawns.

    30.5 VICTORY

        A player wins Chessklerf if:
            -   They capture both of the enemy's corner fortresses, OR
            -   All enemy major pieces are destroyed.

        If both players lose all their major pieces in the same turn
        (for example, a mutual pawn explosion), the game is a draw.
        Nothing matters anymore anyway.


    [ILLUSTRATION: Chessklerf setup diagram. 8x8 board with: two
    corner fortresses on each player's back rank (a1, h1, a8, h8),
    four major pieces on the back rank between them (b1, c1, f1,
    g1 and symmetric for black), pawns on ranks 4 and 5 facing each
    other. Label each piece type and mark the corner fortresses.]



================================================================================
                         PART VI - CAMPAIGN SCENARIOS
================================================================================


    The following scenarios are for Campaign Mode (Section 29). Each
    one runs in 4 acts and can be played in a single session or
    broken into multiple sessions. Read the whole scenario before
    running it.



SCENARIO A: THE NUMBERS STATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Somewhere in the district, a transmitter is still broadcasting.
    Nobody is sure what it's saying anymore, and nobody is sure who
    is transmitting. What is sure is that the people who hear it
    start behaving wrong.

    The party found out about it through a courier. A woman on a
    bicycle, wheels wrapped in duct tape, riding hard from the
    direction of the tower. She skidded to a stop in front of them,
    handed over a tape recorder still warm from her pocket, and
    said "make it stop" with hollow eyes and a pale face. Then she got
    back on the bicycle and rode off the same way she had come, toward the tower.

    The tape is blank. The recorder is still running.

    SETUP

    The players begin this scenario outside the broadcast district.
    Each character has full HP and their starting loadout. One
    character carries the tape recorder. It cannot be discarded.

    Draw one rare fortress from the deck. This is the Numbers Station.
    Set it aside. Draw 10-20 entities from the deck as the enemy pool
    the DM will deploy over the course of the scenario.

    Throughout the scenario, the tape recorder occasionally plays
    back audio that has not been recorded yet. Use this freely for
    flavor: a scream the party has not screamed, a door closing in
    a room the party has not entered, one of the characters saying
    something they are going to say later in the session.

    ACT I - ENTRANCE

    Objective: Cross the district checkpoint.

    The party approaches a narrow road blocked by stacked cars. Two
    checkpoint guards (pull 2 enemies from the pool) wave them down.
    The guards have the flat eyes. They are polite. They keep asking
    if the party has a permit. They keep asking the same way, with
    the same intonation, as if their voices are being routed through
    something that is not their brain.

    The party may:
        -   Fight them (standard combat, party acts first).
        -   Talk their way through (Normal check, 4 or less on 1d6).
            On success, the guards step aside and stare at a spot
            ten feet behind the party until the party is gone.
            Failure triggers combat with the guards already hostile.
        -   Distract one guard by playing the tape recorder at them.
            The guard becomes catatonic for the rest of the scene.
            The other guard must still be dealt with.

    LOCAL RULE - THE BROADCAST IS EVERYWHERE. Any character who
    spends a whole round in this scenario without attacking, moving,
    or speaking must roll 1d6. On a 1, they begin to hear the
    broadcast clearly. Their Attack is reduced by 1 until the end
    of the next round. If a character fails this roll three times
    in one scenario, they become a listener: they stop taking
    actions and must be carried out by their allies. A carried
    listener cannot fight but takes up a party slot. They recover
    after the scenario if the broadcast is ended.

    ACT II - COMPLICATION

    Objective: Find three power cells to reach the top of the tower.

    The broadcast tower's ground-floor elevator is dead. To climb,
    the party needs three working power cells. There are three
    sources in the district.

    SOURCE 1: A SQUAT CONCRETE BUILDING. A bureaucratic holdout:
    people who still believe they work for a government that no
    longer exists. Draw 1 normal fortress as the building. Place
    3 enemies inside. The bureaucrats have one power cell in a
    locked drawer. They will not give it up without either:
        -   A fortress assault (standard rules).
        -   Paperwork. They will hand over the cell if presented
            with any card with writing on it, treated as a "permit"
            with ceremonial solemnity. The party sacrifices 1 item
            card of their choice, handed to the head bureaucrat.
        -   A Hard check (3 or less) to outtalk them.

    SOURCE 2: A CRASHED AMBULANCE. The ambulance still has its
    engine running. It has been running for weeks. One power cell
    is under the hood, but the ambulance's driver is still in the
    front seat, mostly, and still trying to drive. A Hard check
    to reach the engine compartment without the driver noticing.
    On failure, combat with 1 enemy (the driver, pulled from the
    pool, with +1 HP).

    SOURCE 3: A CHILD. A child on a swingset in an empty park is
    holding the third cell like a toy. The child is calm. The child
    is not a listener but is also not a normal child. The child
    will trade the cell for any shiny item (sacrifice 1 item card).
    Refusing to trade and taking the cell by force is allowed but
    has consequences: at the start of Act IV, the child appears at
    the top of the tower as a hostile entity with Attack 4, HP 4,
    and a special that ignores parries.

    ACT III - COLLAPSE

    Objective: Climb the tower.

    The party climbs a reinforced broadcast tower in 3 stages. At
    each stage, the DM deploys 1 enemy from the pool. The enemies
    are ascending the tower from a different face. At each stage,
    also play the tape recorder to the party and describe something
    they have not yet done: "You hear the sound of your own voice
    screaming at a door. It is not a door you have seen yet."

    The party cannot retreat downward without forfeiting the
    scenario. Listeners have gathered at the bottom of the tower
    by the hundred. They are waiting.

    LOCAL RULE - THE CROWD IS WATCHING. After every stage, the
    crowd below grows visibly. On Stage 2, all enemies gain +1
    Attack. On Stage 3, all enemies gain +1 Attack and +1 HP. The
    broadcast is recruiting.

    SPECIAL EVENT AT STAGE 2: One party member hears a voice over
    the tape recorder that is clearly their own voice, older. It
    says one sentence: a piece of advice, a warning, or an apology.
    The DM invents it on the spot based on what the player has done
    so far. This has no mechanical effect but is mandatory.

    ACT IV - THE FINALE

    Objective: Stop the broadcast.

    At the top of the tower is a single figure at a console. They
    have the flat eyes, but there is something else underneath.
    They look up when the party arrives and smile. The smile is
    almost right. It is almost correct. It is the kind of smile
    you would get from someone who has been practicing from a
    photograph.

    When the operator speaks, they speak in a voice that every
    party member privately recognizes: the voice of someone they
    once loved. Each character hears it differently. The DM asks
    each player to name someone quietly, and then uses that name.

    The operator's stats: Attack 4, HP 6. They have one consumable
    that rolls as a 3-damage attack on any one target. They have
    a special: at the start of each round, one random party member
    must roll 1d6; on a 1 or 2, they cannot attack the operator
    that round, because they cannot bring themselves to.

    The party may:
        -   Defeat the operator in combat.
        -   Convince the operator to shut down the broadcast
            voluntarily. A character may attempt one Desperate
            check (2 or less on 1d6) per scenario. The check
            must be made in-character: the player must say
            something to the operator that the DM finds
            sufficiently true.
        -   Destroy the console instead. This is a 1-turn action
            requiring the character to stand still next to the
            console and ignore all combat. The console has HP 4.
            Any character who destroys the console dies in the
            process. Their name is remembered. Their voice is the
            last one on the tape recorder.

    AFTERMATH

    If the party ends the broadcast, the listeners below the tower
    wake up slowly. Some remember who they are. Some do not. Over
    the next days, most drift away from the tower and go home, if
    they have one. Some do not.

    The tape recorder stops playing. It is just a tape recorder
    now. The party can keep it or leave it.



SCENARIO B: THE MEAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    FLAVOR

    You awake and all around you is white. Not a room. Not fog. Not
    snow. Just white, in every direction, with no floor and no ceiling
    and no walls, and you standing in the middle of it like a thought
    someone hasn't finished thinking yet.

    Then the crack.

    It starts small, a hairline fracture in the white, and then the
    white splits apart and you are falling. You fall for what feels
    like hours. You fall long enough to get bored of falling. You fall
    long enough to start wondering if falling is just what you do now.

    Then you hit something hard and hot and everything changes.

    The heat hits your face first. A blinding, searing, greasy heat
    that smells like breakfast. You push yourself up and your hands
    press into something slick and warm and yellow. You are standing
    in a yolk. A yolk the size of a parking lot, trembling slightly,
    stretched out across a frying pan that extends in every direction
    to a horizon that curves upward like the lip of a bowl.

    Around you, others are climbing to their feet. They came from the
    same egg. They look as confused as you feel. The white of the egg
    stretches out behind you like a rubber desert. The pan beneath it
    all is black and enormous and radiating heat from below in waves
    that make the air shimmer.

    In the distance, past the yolk, past the white, past the curved
    edge of the pan, you can see something: a timer. A kitchen timer,
    the size of a building, ticking. You cannot read the numbers from
    here but you can hear it. Every tick vibrates through the pan and
    up through your feet.

    When the timer reaches zero, whoever set it is coming back. And
    they are hungry. And you are breakfast.

    Unless you are not.

    The goal is simple. Make a meal before you become one. If you can
    prepare something worth eating before the timer runs out, whatever
    enormous thing is coming back to eat you will eat that instead. If
    you cannot, you will be served sunny-side up on a plate you will
    never see the edges of, and that will be the end of you.

    SETUP

    Each character begins with:
        -   Their base entity card (character sheet)
        -   0 items
        -   HP at maximum

    SPECIAL SETUP - THE MEAL PLAN. Before the scenario begins, the
    party chooses a difficulty by deciding how many courses they will
    attempt to prepare. More courses means a bigger meal, which means
    a more satisfied creature, which means a better chance of survival.
    It also means more fighting, more scavenging, and less time.

        1-COURSE MEAL (EASY)
            Ingredients needed: 3 entities + 3 items argued as edible
            Serviceware needed: 1 item argued as a utensil, cup, OR plate
            Time: 16 rounds total across the scenario

        2-COURSE MEAL (MEDIUM)
            Ingredients needed: 6 entities + 6 items argued as edible
            Serviceware needed: 2 of the following (no duplicates):
                                utensil, cup, plate
            Time: 12 rounds total across the scenario

        3-COURSE MEAL (HARD)
            Ingredients needed: 9 entities + 9 items argued as edible
            Serviceware needed: all 3: utensil AND cup AND plate
            Time: 10 rounds total across the scenario

        4-COURSE MEAL (IMPOSSIBLE)
            Ingredients needed: 12 entities + 12 items argued as edible
            Serviceware needed: all 3: utensil AND cup AND plate
            Time: 8 rounds total across the scenario

    Each course requires exactly 3 defeated entities and 3 items
    argued as edible, plus the party must collectively explain what
    dish these 6 components make and how they cooked it.

    The DM tracks the round timer. Every combat round and every
    non-combat action round counts toward the timer. When the timer
    reaches zero, the scenario ends, regardless of progress. If the
    meal is incomplete, the party loses.

    THE CORE MECHANIC - ARGUING EDIBILITY

    This scenario runs on persuasion and creativity, not on a fixed
    recipe. The cards in the Goosklerf deck are not labeled as food.
    They are labeled as weapons, armor, creatures, and strange
    objects. But this is a kitchen, and in a kitchen, everything is
    potentially an ingredient.

    When the party defeats an entity or acquires an item, they may
    declare it as one of the following:

        AN INGREDIENT (entity or item). The party must argue to the
        DM that this card could plausibly be construed as edible, or
        as a component of a dish. The argument does not need to be
        good. It needs to be committed.

        Examples of arguments that should work:
            -   "This entity is made of organic matter. We can cook
                 organic matter."
            -   "This shield is basically a tortilla if you squint."
            -   "This potion is a broth. It is a broth now."
            -   "This creature has meat on it. All creatures have
                 meat on them."
            -   "This glowing crystal is rock candy."

        Examples of arguments that should NOT work:
            -   "This sword is food." (It is steel. You cannot eat
                 steel. Try harder.)
            -   "This entity is edible because I said so." (Make a
                 real argument.)
            -   "This helmet is a bowl of soup." (A helmet could be
                 a BOWL. It is not soup. You need to put something
                 IN the helmet.)

        THE DM IS THE JUDGE. The DM decides whether the argument
        passes. The standard is generous but not infinite. If the
        argument makes the table laugh, it passes. If the argument
        makes the table groan, it passes. If the argument makes
        nobody react at all, it probably fails. The DM should err
        on the side of yes.

        IMPORTANT: An item used as an ingredient is consumed. It
        is added to the meal and cannot be used as equipment
        anymore. An entity used as an ingredient is placed in the
        party's ingredient pile. A player may choose to keep a
        useful item as equipment instead of contributing it to the
        meal. This is the central tension: your best weapon might
        also be your best ingredient.

    A UTENSIL, CUP, OR PLATE (item only). The party must also
    argue that certain items serve as serviceware. The creature
    coming back needs something to eat WITH, something to drink
    FROM, and something to eat OFF OF.

        UTENSIL: Any item that could plausibly be used to pick up,
        cut, stab, scoop, or otherwise move food from a plate to
        a mouth. A sword is a knife. A staff is a skewer. A shield
        on a stick is a spatula. Tongs are tongs.

        CUP: Any item that could plausibly hold liquid. A helmet
        is a cup. A hollowed-out shield is a cup. A boot is a cup
        if you are desperate enough and argue with conviction.

        PLATE: Any item with a flat or concave surface large
        enough to hold a meal. A shield is a plate. A breastplate
        is a plate. A book opened flat is a plate if you do not
        care about the book.

    Items designated as serviceware are NOT consumed. They are
    placed at the table setting in Act III and remain there.
    An item cannot be both an ingredient and serviceware. Pick one.

    STARTING EQUIPMENT. Each character draws 1 random item from
    the deck. This is their only starting gear. Whatever the card
    depicts, in this scenario it is also whatever the party can
    argue it is. A sword is a knife. A potion is a marinade. A
    helmet is a salad bowl. The card keeps its normal stats for
    combat purposes regardless of what the party calls it.

    ENEMY POOL. Set aside 15 entities from the deck, face-down.
    These are deployed by the DM over the course of the scenario.
    Remaining entities not used by the end of the scenario are
    wasted. The party does not need to fight every enemy, only
    enough to meet their course requirements.

    ACT I - THE PAN

    Objective: Get your bearings and reach the counter.

    The party stands in the yolk. The heat is rising. The surface
    of the pan stretches out around them, and the air smells like
    oil and metal and something browning at the edges.

    The yolk is cooking. The party can feel it firming up beneath
    their feet. They have a limited window before the surface they
    are standing on becomes solid and they become part of the dish.

    LOCAL RULE - THE SIZZLE. At the end of every round spent on
    the pan surface (Acts I and III), each character must roll 1d6.
    On a 1, the heat gets to them: they take 1 damage. Characters
    standing on an item card designated as an ingredient (using it
    as a platform, not yet contributed to the meal) are immune to
    the Sizzle for that round.

    The party must cross the yolk and the egg white to reach the
    edge of the pan. This requires 3 rounds of movement. During
    the crossing, 1 enemy appears from the egg white. Pull 1
    entity from the enemy pool. This enemy is rubbery, translucent,
    and deeply offended at being cracked open.

    If the party defeats this entity, they may immediately argue
    it as their first ingredient. It is, after all, egg. That
    argument should be easy.

    At the edge of the pan, the party can see the counter. The
    counter is a vast plain of tile stretching away from the
    stovetop, littered with enormous objects: a salt shaker the
    size of a grain silo, a pepper mill like a lighthouse, a
    block of knives standing upright like monoliths.

    To get off the pan, each character must make a Normal check
    (4 or less on 1d6) to climb the lip of the pan without
    burning themselves. Failure: take 1 damage but still make it
    over. No one gets left behind on the pan.

    ACT II - THE COUNTER

    Objective: Gather ingredients, serviceware, and weapons.

    The countertop is a hostile environment. Everything here is
    sized for someone who is not the party's size. A puddle of
    olive oil is a lake. A dishrag is a landscape. A sponge is a
    building with a disturbing texture.

    The party explores the counter in search of things to fight,
    things to cook, and things to cook with. The DM structures
    the counter as a series of STATIONS. Each station costs 1
    round of travel to reach.

    At each station, TWO things happen:

        1. COMBAT. Pull 1 entity from the enemy pool. Combat is
           resolved normally. If the party defeats the entity,
           they may argue it as an ingredient (the DM judges the
           argument on the spot). If they cannot make a convincing
           case, the defeated entity is wasted. Not everything is
           food. Try harder next time.

        2. SCAVENGING. After combat (or instead of combat, if
           the party chooses to flee the station), each character
           at the station may spend 1 action to scavenge. Draw 1
           random item from the deck. The character keeps the item
           and may use it as equipment, or may immediately argue
           it as an ingredient or serviceware.

    THE DM SHOULD FLAVOR EACH STATION. Suggested locations on
    the counter for the DM to describe and improvise from:

        THE CUTTING BOARD. A scarred wooden plateau. Knife marks
        everywhere. Remnants of whatever was chopped here last.
        Good scavenging potential: items found here are more
        likely to be arguable as utensils or cutting implements.
        The entity here fights with sharp, chopping motions.

        THE SINK. A vast chrome basin with a dripping faucet the
        size of a waterfall. Wet. Slippery. Characters at the
        sink take -1 to attack rolls (bad footing). But any item
        scavenged here can be argued as "washed and therefore
        more plausibly edible," giving the player a +1 bonus to
        their edibility argument (DM should be more lenient).

        THE SPICE RACK. A vertical cliff of enormous jars. No
        combat at this station (it is peaceful here, somehow).
        Each character may scavenge 1 item. Any item found at
        the spice rack automatically counts as edible with no
        argument required. It is a spice. It came from the spice
        rack. That is the argument and it is unimpeachable. The
        DM may limit visits to the spice rack to once per
        scenario.

        THE TRASH CAN. The rim of a metal bin, overflowing with
        debris. 2 enemies at this station instead of 1 (things
        that live in garbage are territorial). Scavenged items
        here are harder to argue as edible (the DM should be
        stricter; "it came from the trash" is a fair counter-
        argument). But scavenging is plentiful: draw 2 items
        instead of 1.

        THE STOVETOP. Another burner, not the one the party woke
        up on. Still hot. The Sizzle rule applies here. The enemy
        at this station has +1 Attack (the heat makes everything
        angrier). But any entity defeated here is automatically
        arguable as "already cooked," which is a strong argument.

        THE FRIDGE. If the party reaches the fridge (requires 2
        rounds of travel instead of 1 — it is far from the
        stovetop), they find it open. Inside is cold and dark
        and full of old things. No combat. Each character may
        scavenge 2 items. Anything from the fridge is
        automatically arguable as edible (it was in the fridge,
        so at some point someone considered it food). But on a
        roll of 1 on 1d6 per item, the item is "expired." An
        expired ingredient can still be contributed to a course,
        but the creature will spit that course out during the
        tasting in Act IV (the DM reveals this only then, and
        the course does not count). Players do not learn which
        items are expired until the tasting.

    THE TIMER IS VISIBLE. Remind the party of the timer at the
    end of every round. They can hear it ticking. It is getting
    louder.

    ARGUING HAPPENS IN REAL TIME. When a player argues that an
    entity is edible or that an item is a utensil, the argument
    happens at the table, out loud, to the DM. Other players may
    support or challenge the argument. The DM rules. This is the
    scenario's signature mechanic and should be encouraged to be
    theatrical. A player who stands up and passionately defends
    the edibility of a shield boss should be rewarded for
    commitment.

    The party may designate items as serviceware at any point
    during Acts II or III. Serviceware does not need to be argued
    at the moment of acquisition. A player may hold an item and
    decide later whether it is a weapon, an ingredient, or a
    plate.

    ACT III - THE PREP

    Objective: Return to the pan, cook the meal, set the table.

    The party must return to the stovetop with their collected
    ingredients and serviceware and actually cook. The pan they
    woke up on is their cooking station. It is still hot and
    still has the remains of the egg on it.

    RETURNING TO THE PAN. Each character makes a Normal check to
    descend back onto the pan safely. Failure: 1 damage.

    THE COOKING PHASE. For each course, the party must do the
    following:

        1. LAY OUT THE INGREDIENTS. Place the 3 entity cards and
           3 item cards designated as edible for this course in
           a visible pile on the table.

        2. NAME THE DISH. The party must, out loud, name the dish
           they are making from these 6 components. The name
           should be specific. "Food" is not a dish name. "Pan-
           Seared Golem Flank with Shield-Crust Garnish and a
           Potion Reduction" is a dish name.

        3. DESCRIBE THE COOKING. The party must explain to the DM
           how they are cooking the dish. This includes:
               -   What method they are using (frying, boiling,
                   roasting, assembling raw, smoking, etc.)
               -   Which ingredients are cooked and which are
                   served raw or cold
               -   Any preparation steps (chopping, marinating,
                   seasoning, etc.)
               -   How the components work together as a dish

           The DM judges the cooking explanation by the same
           standard as the edibility arguments: committed and
           creative beats technically accurate. If the party
           describes their cooking process with conviction and
           at least a passing relationship to how food works,
           it passes. If they just say "we cook it," they need
           to try harder.

        4. COOK. One character must spend 1 round per course
           actively cooking. Roll a Normal check (4 or less on
           1d6):

           Success: The course is prepared.

           Failure: The course is underdone. Spend 1 additional
           round and try again. No check needed on the retry;
           it just takes time. Time you may not have.

        5. PLATE AND SET. Once all courses are prepared, the
           party must lay out the serviceware. Place the utensil,
           cup, and/or plate items (as required by difficulty)
           next to the completed courses. Describe the table
           setting briefly. This costs 1 round total, regardless
           of how many serviceware items are placed.

    THE KITCHEN FIGHTS BACK. During the cooking phase, the DM
    deploys 1 final enemy from the enemy pool (or invents one if
    the pool is empty). This enemy is the kitchen itself: a
    sentient burner flame, a vengeful oven mitt, a grease
    splatter with eyes and opinions. It attacks the party while
    they are trying to cook. The party must fight it while
    continuing to prepare courses. Characters not currently in
    combat may continue cooking.

    LOCAL RULE - MULTITASKING. A character may attempt to both
    fight and cook in the same round by splitting their action:
    roll for the attack at -1 Attack, AND roll for the cooking
    check, both in the same round. If the attack misses, the
    cooking also fails (you dropped the spatula). If the attack
    hits, the cooking succeeds normally.

    SPECIAL EVENT. When the party finishes plating the final
    course and setting the serviceware, the meal is complete.
    The pan begins to glow. The smell changes from "you are
    being cooked" to "something is being cooked for someone."
    The timer, in the distance, stops ticking. There is a
    moment of silence.

    Then the shadow falls.

    ACT IV - THE PLATE

    Objective: Survive the serving.

    A hand descends from above. It is enormous. It belongs to
    something that is not entirely visible: you can see the
    fingers, the wrist, and then the arm disappears into a
    height your eyes refuse to process. Whatever this thing is,
    it built this kitchen, and it cracked the egg you came from,
    and it set the timer, and now it is back.

    If the party completed the meal before the timer ran out:

        THE TASTING. The creature examines the meal. It picks up
        the utensil the party designated. It inspects the plate.
        It looks at each course.

        For each course the party prepared, the DM secretly rolls
        1d6:

            1: The creature is DISGUSTED. This course does not
               count. (If the party used an expired fridge
               ingredient, this result is automatic for that
               course.) The creature makes a sound that shakes
               the kitchen.

            2-3: The creature is UNIMPRESSED but eats the course
               anyway. This course counts.

            4-5: The creature ENJOYS the course. This course
               counts. The creature makes a satisfied noise.

            6: The creature is DELIGHTED. This course counts
               double for scoring purposes. The creature pauses,
               looks down at the pan, and for a moment seems to
               notice the party. It does not eat them. It nods,
               very slightly, the way a restaurant patron nods
               at a chef through the kitchen window.

        After all courses are tasted, count how many courses
        were accepted (scored 2 or higher). If at least HALF
        the courses the party attempted are accepted, the
        creature is satisfied. It takes the plate and the
        serviceware, and it leaves. The party survives.

        If fewer than half are accepted, the creature is still
        hungry. It looks at the pan. It looks at the party. The
        party has 1 round to offer something else: any remaining
        items argued as edible, any remaining entity cards not
        yet used, anything. If the party can offer at least 2
        more cards argued as edible on the spot (with arguments
        the DM accepts), the creature accepts the extra offering
        and leaves. If they cannot, proceed to the failure
        condition below.

        ESCAPE. If the creature leaves satisfied, each character
        makes one final Normal check to climb off the pan and
        reach the edge of the counter. From there, they find a
        crack in the wall behind the stove: a gap in reality,
        the same kind of crack that dropped them here. They step
        through.

        They wake up somewhere else. It is not the kitchen. It
        may or may not be better. But they are alive, and they
        are not breakfast, and that is more than they expected
        when they woke up inside an egg.

    If the party did NOT complete the meal before the timer ran
    out, or the creature was not satisfied:

        The hand takes the pan. All of it. The party included.
        They feel the pan tilt. They feel themselves slide. The
        last thing they see is a plate, white and enormous and
        perfectly clean, rushing up to meet them.

        Total party defeat. The game ends.

        The DM is encouraged to describe the plating with the
        same care and dignity one would describe a funeral. It
        is, after all, the same thing.

    AFTERMATH AND SCORING

        If the party survived:

            Each surviving character:                    25 points
            Each accepted course:                       50 points
            Each delighted course (rolled 6):          100 points
            Meal completed with 2+ rounds to spare:     50 points
            No character reached 0 HP:                  50 points
            Each unused ingredient remaining:            10 points

        If the party was eaten:

            0 points. You are brunch.

    DM NOTES

        THE ARGUING IS THE GAME. The edibility arguments and the
        dish descriptions are the signature mechanic of this
        scenario. Encourage players to be theatrical. Reward
        creativity and commitment. A player who describes, with
        total conviction, how they braise a crystal golem into a
        mineral jus should be rewarded for that performance. A
        player who just says "this is food now" should be asked
        to try harder.

        The DM's role as judge is critical. Be generous with
        organic or biological entities (anything with a body
        probably has meat on it). Be generous with liquids and
        potions (they are sauces, broths, marinades, reductions).
        Be strict with pure metal or stone (a sword is not food;
        a sword is a knife, and a knife is a utensil, but the
        sword itself cannot be eaten). Be flexible with the
        weird middle ground (a wooden shield could be a cutting
        board or a plate, but could it be edible? Only if the
        player commits to the argument that it is a fiber-rich
        cracker).

        The tension between using a card as a weapon vs. an
        ingredient is the scenario's core resource dilemma. The
        party's best combat item might also be the ingredient
        that completes their second course. They must choose.

        The cooking description is not a skill check. It is a
        performance. The DM should treat it like a contestant
        presenting their dish on a cooking show: what did you
        make, how did you make it, why does it work? If the
        player can answer those three questions with a straight
        face, the dish is valid.

        The timer is the real antagonist, not the ingredients.
        Keep it visible. Keep it audible. Every time a player
        wastes a round, remind them how many ticks are left.

        If a character dies during this scenario, describe them
        as having been "fully cooked." They are no longer a
        party member. They are a side dish. This is very sad
        and also very funny and the DM should commit to both
        emotions simultaneously.

        THE CREATURE IS NEVER FULLY DESCRIBED. The party sees
        a hand, an arm, a shadow. They hear chewing. They feel
        the kitchen shake when it walks. They never see its
        face. This is intentional. Whatever the creature is, it
        is beyond the scale of the scenario. It is not a boss
        to be fought. It is weather. It is gravity. It is the
        thing that decides whether you are a chef or a meal, and
        it does not owe you an explanation.


SCENARIO C: THE GROCERY RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The store is still standing. That alone is miraculous. It still
    has fluorescent lights. It still has shelves. The freezer
    section still hums. No one knows why. No one wants to ask.

    The store is called BIG VALUE. Some of the letters in the sign
    are out, so depending on the angle and the time of day it reads
    BIG VAL or B  VALUE or, occasionally, just E. When the store reads E, a disembodied
    head can be seen floating around, spouting nonsense. The parking lot
    is full of cars that have not moved in months. The cars are
    full of things that used to be drivers.

    The party is hungry. The store will have food. Other parties are
    also hungry. They will also be at the store. There will not be
    enough for everyone. The question is how much of yourself you
    are willing to leave at the checkout.

    SETUP

    Each character starts with reduced HP (current HP = maximum HP
    minus 1). This is hunger. It does not heal until the party eats
    actual food at any point during the scenario, after which all
    characters return to full HP.

    Draw 1 normal fortress from the deck as the store. Set aside 6
    entities as a pool of rival scavengers and store oddities.

    Take a small handful of item cards (around 8) and set them
    face-down as the store's stock. These will be drawn during the
    shopping action in Act II.

    Throughout this scenario, the store's PA system occasionally
    plays announcements. Use these freely as flavor: a price check
    on aisle 4 for an item that does not exist, a reminder that the
    store closes at 9 (the clock is broken and reads 9 permanently),
    a manager being called to the front, a manager being called to
    the back, a manager being called to a location with no name.
    There is no manager. There has not been a manager for a long
    time.

    ACT I - ENTRANCE

    Objective: Reach the store parking lot.

    The party moves through 3 empty streets toward BIG VALUE. At
    each street, roll 1d6 and resolve the result:

        1: A rival scavenger lurking in a doorway. Pull 1 enemy
           from the pool. Combat.
        2: A car with a passenger still strapped in, asking the
           party for directions in a polite voice. The passenger
           is not hostile. The passenger does not want directions.
           Players may ignore them, talk to them (DM improvises),
           or destroy the car (no reward, but it stops the voice).
        3: A pile of grocery bags abandoned mid-street, contents
           rotted. A character may search them for 1 random item.
           On a roll of 1, the bag is occupied.
        4: Nothing. Wind. A dog barking somewhere. The dog is
           saying words but not loudly enough to make out.
        5: A dead radio playing music that has not been written
           yet. No mechanical effect.
        6: A useful find. Draw 1 random item from the deck and
           give it to any party member.

    After the third street, the party can see the store. The
    parking lot is full. The lights are on. The doors slide open
    automatically as if welcoming them. They keep sliding open and
    closed even after the party stops moving.

    ACT II - COMPLICATION

    Objective: Get inside and load up.

    The store is held by 2 rival entities (pull from the pool) who
    have set up a checkpoint at the entrance. They are wearing red
    polo shirts they have stolen from the staff section and they
    are pretending very hard to be employees. They claim the store
    is closed for inventory. They are waiting for someone they will
    not name.

    The party may:
        -   Fight them at the entrance (standard combat).
        -   Comply with the inventory checkpoint by handing over
            1 item. The fake employees will let the party pass and
            will continue pretending until the party is out of
            sight.
        -   Hide and wait them out via a Hard check (3 or less).
            Success means slipping past unnoticed. Failure means
            combat with the fake employees, who attack first.
        -   Distract them by triggering a separate disturbance:
            knocking over a shopping cart corral, setting off the
            store alarm, etc. Allow a Normal check. On success, the
            fake employees investigate and the party slips by.

    Once inside, the store is real. Mostly. The shelves are mostly
    full. Many of the products have labels that are wrong: brand
    names spelled differently, photographs of foods the photograph
    is not actually depicting, expiration dates in years that have
    not happened. The fluorescent lights flicker only when looked
    at directly.

    SHOPPING. A character may spend one action to shop. They draw
    1 face-down item from the store stock pile. Each character may
    shop at most 3 times in this act.

    Each time a character shops, after drawing the item, roll 1d6:
        1: The aisle moves. The character finds themselves in a
           different aisle than they intended. They may shop again
           on their next action without it counting against the
           3-shop limit, but they are now separated from the party
           until they make a Normal check to find their way back.
        2: Something on a high shelf is watching. The DM describes
           it but does not attack. Yet.
        3-5: Normal shopping.
        6: Bonus. Draw a second item.

    EATING. At any point during Act II, the party may eat. This
    requires sacrificing 1 item card from the store stock (treat
    it as food regardless of what it actually is). Eating restores
    all characters to full HP. The party may only eat once per
    scenario. Eating is loud and the party is vulnerable for one
    round afterward (-1 to all attack rolls).

    AT THE BACK OF THE STORE, behind the swinging double doors
    marked EMPLOYEES ONLY, there is a freezer. The freezer is
    locked. The freezer is also vibrating slightly, in a rhythm
    that is almost a heartbeat but slower. Curious characters may
    investigate. Opening the freezer requires a Hard check or
    breaking the lock (any item used as a tool, sacrifice that
    item). Inside is one rare item card, drawn from the deck. Also
    inside is something the DM invents on the spot and the party
    does not have time to look at carefully. Whatever it is, it
    sees the party. The party should leave.

    ACT III - COLLAPSE

    Objective: Escape before rival scavengers arrive.

    Three waves of rival scavengers converge on the store. They
    have heard the party is here. Each wave is 2 enemies pulled
    from the pool. The waves enter from different sides:

        Wave 1: Through the front sliding doors.
        Wave 2: Through the loading dock at the back of the store.
        Wave 3: From above. The DM does not need to explain how.

    The party may:
        -   Defend the store. Use the store fortress as their
            position. The store fortress grants +1 Attack and +1 HP
            to defenders inside it for the duration of Act III.
            Survive all three waves to advance.
        -   Flee toward home, dropping items to move faster. Each
            item voluntarily dropped grants the dropping character
            +1 to the next round's actions. Dropped items are
            permanently lost.
        -   Split the party, with some defending and some fleeing.
            Allowed but costly: split parties roll separately for
            everything in Act IV.

    LOCAL RULE - GREED IS HEAVY. Any character carrying more than
    2 items takes a -1 penalty to all attack rolls and a -1 penalty
    to all checks while fleeing in Act III. The store's gravity
    seems to increase the more you take from it.

    SPECIAL EVENT DURING WAVE 2: The PA system makes an
    announcement, addressing the party by name, even though no one
    in this scenario knows their names. The announcement
    congratulates them on their selections and reminds them that
    the express checkout is for 10 items or fewer. Then the lights
    in the store get slightly brighter for one round. During that
    round, all characters and enemies have +1 to their Attack
    rolls. The store wants combat. The store is enjoying this.

    ACT IV - THE FINALE

    Objective: Make it home.

    The party retreats from the store with whatever they still
    carry. The walk back is shorter than the walk in, even though
    it is the same distance. The streets feel like they are
    helping.

    Each surviving character must make 1 Normal check (4 or less
    on 1d6) to reach the safehouse. Modifiers:
        -   +1 difficulty (easier) if the character is carrying 2
            or fewer items.
        -   -1 difficulty (harder) if the character is carrying 4
            or more items.
        -   -1 difficulty if the character ate during Act II.
        -   The character who opened the freezer in Act II must
            make their check at Hard (3 or less). Whatever was in
            there has decided to follow them home. They will not
            see it. They will hear it walking behind them with the
            same number of footsteps no matter how fast they run.

    Characters who fail the check are caught in an alley by a
    final rival pulled from the pool. They must fight 1 enemy
    solo. Surviving the fight completes the check.

    AFTERMATH

    Victory is measured by items successfully brought home across
    all surviving characters.

        7 or more items: A great haul. The party eats well for a
                         week. Each character permanently regains
                         1 HP up to their max if they have lost any
                         since the campaign started.
        4 to 6 items:    A good haul. The party eats. That is
                         enough.
        1 to 3 items:    A meager haul. The party eats once and
                         remembers being hungry the next day.
        0 items:         No haul. But everyone who came back came
                         back, which is more than most parties can
                         say. That counts.

    The character who opened the freezer hears something at their
    safehouse window that night. It is the sound of breathing from
    the wrong side of the glass. It is gone in the morning. Or
    it is not.

SCENARIO D: THE LAB
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    You wake up in a hallway you do not remember. There is tile
    under your face. Fluorescent light. A number printed on the
    doorframe that you are certain was different a moment ago. You
    look at it. It changes again. It is now your birthday, written
    backwards, in a font you have never seen.

    You have no weapons. Your clothes are not your clothes. They
    are the right size but they fit wrong, the way a stranger's
    shirt fits even when it is your size. Around your wrist is a
    plastic hospital bracelet with a number on it, and below the
    number, a name. The name is not yours, but you recognize it.
    You are almost sure it is the name of someone you know. You
    cannot remember who.

    A voice somewhere down the corridor is reading a list, very
    slowly, and one of the names on the list is yours. Or it is
    going to be. You cannot tell from here.

    The party does not know who they are, how they got here, or
    what happens to the people whose names get read aloud. Everyone
    agrees this is a bad sign.

    SETUP

    Each character begins with:
        -   Their base entity card (their character sheet)
        -   0 items
        -   HP at maximum
        -   A wrist bracelet (the throughline prop, see below)

    THE WRIST BRACELETS. At the start of the scenario, each player
    is told their character is wearing a hospital bracelet with a
    number and a name on it. The DM secretly assigns each character
    a bracelet name from a list. The list is the names of the OTHER
    characters in the party. Every character is wearing a bracelet
    that says someone else's name.

    The players do not learn this until the bracelets become
    relevant in Act II and Act IV. Until then, treat the bracelet
    as flavor: the character can read their bracelet, the name on
    it is a person they almost remember, and that is all.

    Draw 1 super fortress to be the Core Lab. Set aside 8 entities
    as a pool of "experiments" - things the lab has been making.
    These should not be presented as monsters at first. They should
    be presented as employees, patients, doctors, or visitors who
    are slightly wrong.

    Throughout the scenario, intercom announcements address the
    characters not by name but by their bracelet number. Use
    these freely as flavor: a request for Patient 14 to report to
    Observation, an apology that the cafeteria is closed today,
    a routine reminder that all personnel are reminded that
    contamination protocols are in effect on Sublevel 3, the
    cheerful daily affirmation read by a voice that is
    unmistakably trying its first day at being a voice.

    ACT I - ENTRANCE

    Objective: Escape your containment cell.

    Each character starts alone in a separate cell on the same
    corridor. They cannot see each other but they can hear each
    other if they shout. The cells have small windows in the doors,
    looking out onto the corridor, but the windows are clouded.

    Act I is played by each player in turn while the others wait.

    Each character must escape their cell. Two paths exist:

    PATH A - THE PHYSICAL ESCAPE. Make two Hard checks (3 or less
    on 1d6) in sequence. The first check finds something useful
    in the cell to pry with: a loose tile, a bedframe spring, a
    plastic spoon that has been sharpened by the previous
    occupant. The second check uses the tool to defeat the door
    latch.

    PATH B - THE COOPERATIVE ESCAPE. The cells are connected by
    air vents. Two characters whose players agree may attempt a
    cooperative escape. Each makes one Normal check (4 or less).
    If both succeed, they crawl out together. If one fails, both
    must make a Hard check or be discovered. The cooperative
    escape is faster but louder.

    A character who fails all attempts is discovered by a
    patrolling lab entity (pull 1 from the pool, presented as an
    orderly with a clipboard and very specific questions). They
    must defeat the orderly to escape.

    AT THE END OF ACT I: All escaped characters meet in a central
    corridor. The corridor has a wall map of the facility on it.
    The map is hand-drawn in pencil and looks like it was made
    by someone who only had access to the building one room at a
    time. Some rooms are labeled. Some are crossed out. One room
    is labeled with the name of a character who is currently
    standing in the corridor, looking at the map.

    Each character draws 1 random item from the deck. These were
    in a supply closet near the meeting point.

    ACT II - COMPLICATION

    Objective: Find your weapons, your names, and three keycards.

    The party must explore 3 lab rooms. Each room has a door with
    a different label, and each label is wrong in a different way.
    Suggested labels for the DM (use any, in any order):

        -   "JANITORIAL" (this is not a janitor's closet)
        -   "CAFETERIA - CLOSED FOR REMODEL" (it has not been a
            cafeteria for a long time)
        -   "OBSERVATION ROOM 4" (the observation goes both ways)
        -   "PHYSICAL THERAPY" (it is not therapy)
        -   "WAITING ROOM" (something is waiting)

    For each room the party enters:
        -   Draw 1 entity from the pool as the room's threat.
            Present it not as a monster but as something that
            still thinks it has a job.
        -   Draw 1 item as the room's loot.
        -   Roll 1d6 for the room's reality stability:

            1: STAT BLEED. One random character swaps one stat
               value (Attack and HP swap) until they leave the
               room. They may take this opportunity to discover
               this is sometimes useful.
            2: INVERTED PHARMACOLOGY. All item effects used in
               this room are inverted (heals damage, buffs become
               debuffs, defensive items become aggressive ones).
               The DM should narrate the inversion in-character:
               the bandage tightens too much, the painkiller
               sharpens the senses to the point of agony.
            3: ECHO ROOM. The room repeats itself once after you
               leave it. Fight the threat again, but the second
               time it knows your strategy and acts accordingly
               (DM picks better tactics).
            4: NORMAL. Nothing unusual happens. This is in some
               ways more disturbing.
            5: NORMAL. As above.
            6: MEMORY FRAGMENT. A character remembers a fragment
               of their identity. They gain +1 maximum HP
               permanently. The fragment should be specific: the
               smell of their grandmother's kitchen, the feel of
               their first car's steering wheel, the sound of a
               particular person laughing. The DM should ask the
               player to describe it.

    KEYCARDS. Each room contains 1 keycard, hidden somewhere
    appropriate to the room (in a drawer, under a body, on a
    lanyard around the neck of the threat, etc.). The party may
    or may not find each keycard. Each room with a found keycard
    grants the party 1 keycard for Act III.

    THE BRACELETS BECOME RELEVANT. Somewhere in Act II, ideally
    in the second room, the DM should describe a wall of patient
    files: photographs paperclipped to manila folders, with names
    and bracelet numbers. The players are encouraged to read their
    own bracelets aloud. They will discover their bracelets have
    other party members' names on them.

    The DM should not explain this. The DM should let the players
    sit with it for as long as it takes.

    ACT III - COLLAPSE

    Objective: Reach the Core Lab.

    The party reaches the central hub of the facility. The Core
    Lab is on the other side of a heavy security door that
    requires 3 keycards. The hallway leading to it is wider than
    the rest of the facility, ceiling tiles missing, the lights
    flickering in a pattern that is almost rhythmic, almost
    musical.

    If the party has 3 keycards, they enter directly. For each
    missing keycard, the party must make a Desperate check (2 or
    less on 1d6). Failure means fighting 1 additional entity from
    the pool as a guardian.

    LOCAL RULE - THE HALLWAY IS LEAKING. At the start of each
    round inside the hallway, roll 1d6:

        1: One random character temporarily forgets one of their
           items. The item cannot be used for that round only.
           The character also cannot describe it: they know it
           is in their pack, they know it is important, but the
           word for it has gone out of their mouth.
        2: One random character sees themselves at the end of
           the hallway, walking ahead of the party, looking
           back. The figure waves. Then it is gone. No mechanical
           effect.
        3: A door opens in the wall that was not there a moment
           ago. Inside the doorway is a room. The room is the
           bedroom of one random character, exactly as they left
           it the morning of the reality collapse. The door
           closes again at the start of the next round. A
           character may step through during the open round
           (sacrificing all actions that round) to retrieve 1
           item from their bedroom: any 1 item card from the
           graveyard, restored.
        4-6: Nothing.

    SPECIAL EVENT NEAR THE END OF THE HALLWAY: The party
    encounters a single figure standing motionless, facing the
    door to the Core Lab. The figure is wearing a lab coat and
    a wrist bracelet. The bracelet has, written on it, the name
    of a real person from the players' actual lives. The DM
    should ask each player to name a person they trust, and use
    one of those names. The figure does not respond when spoken
    to. The figure does not attack. If the party attacks first,
    the figure dies in one hit, with no resistance, and the
    party feels slightly worse afterward but continues. If the
    party ignores it and continues to the Core Lab, the figure
    is still standing there when they look back. Then it is gone.

    ACT IV - THE FINALE

    Objective: Destroy or seize the distortion machine.

    The Core Lab is the super fortress set aside at setup.

    Inside it is the machine. The machine is large and quiet. It
    does not look like science. It looks like furniture, the kind
    of furniture you find in a room that has been waiting for
    someone for a very long time. There is a chair in front of
    it. The chair has restraints. The restraints are open.

    Standing next to the machine is the boss: an entity with
    Attack 5, HP 8, and a consumable that rolls as a 2-damage
    attack on all attackers in the engagement. The boss is
    wearing a lab coat. The boss's wrist bracelet, when the party
    sees it, has the name of one of the party members on it.

    THE BRACELET REVELATION. Before the fight begins, the DM
    should tell the players what they have begun to suspect:
    each character is wearing a bracelet with the name of one
    of the others. The boss is wearing the name of one specific
    party member: the DM picks. That party member is the only
    one in the party whose bracelet does NOT have someone in
    the party on it. Their bracelet has the boss's name. They
    have always had it. They just have not read it carefully.

    The implication is left for the party to interpret. The DM
    should not explain it. Some possibilities:
        -   They are all the same person being tested.
        -   They have all been switched.
        -   The bracelets do not mean anything and the lab is
            just trying to make them think they do.
        -   They are all someone else, looking back.

    The party may:
        -   Destroy the machine. Defeat the boss in combat,
            then assault the fortress (the Core Lab) until it
            reaches 0 HP. This ends the scenario in destruction:
            the lab collapses, the party escapes, and the
            distortions in this district end. The bracelets
            crumble to dust as the party leaves.
        -   Seize the machine. Defeat the boss, but leave the
            fortress standing. The first character to touch the
            machine after the boss dies gains +1 Attack
            permanently but is now connected to it. At the
            start of every future scenario, that character must
            roll 1d6. On a 1, they glitch: lose 1 HP permanently
            and lose 1 turn at the start of the next combat.
        -   Sit in the chair. One character may volunteer to
            sit in the chair after the boss is defeated. The
            chair will not let them up. The machine will start
            doing whatever it does. The character is gone at
            the end of the scenario, but the rest of the party
            walks out clean, with no penalty, and one specific
            thing about the world is restored: the DM picks
            something small and personal to one of the
            remaining party members. A grandmother is alive
            again. A song plays on a radio that has been silent.
            A dog can no longer talk, and is happier for it.

    AFTERMATH

    Escape from the lab is automatic for all surviving characters.
    The corridors that took hours to traverse on the way in take
    minutes on the way out. The doors are unlocked. The
    fluorescent lights have begun to fail one by one as the party
    walks out. The voice on the intercom has gone silent.

    Outside, the sun is rising. None of the party can quite agree
    on whether the sun was rising when they went in. None of them
    can quite agree on how long it has been.

    The character who sat in the chair, if any, is not with the
    party when they reach the parking lot. None of the others can
    quite remember them anymore. Their items are still in the
    party's packs. Their name is still on someone's bracelet. That
    bracelet does not crumble.

    You may or may not find out who you were before this started.
    You probably won't. But you know who the others are now, and
    you know they came back for you, and that is more than most
    people in this world get to keep.

GLOSSARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    ACT             One major segment of a Campaign Mode scenario.
                    Scenarios have 4 acts.

    ATTACK          A card's target number for normal hits. Roll this
                    value or less on 1d6 to land a hit.

    BATTLEFIELD     The central shared zone of the table where most
                    combat takes place.

    BATTLEPATH      The sequence of face-down enemy fortresses in
                    Singleplayer Mode.

    CAMPAIGN MODE   The RPG mode of Goosklerf II, run by a DM.

    CAPTURE         Taking control of an enemy fortress by clearing
                    it of defenders via a battlefield assault. At
                    least one entity must be garrisoned in the
                    captured fortress for control to transfer.

    COMBAT ROUND    One alternating exchange in combat (attacker
                    actions, then defender actions). An engagement
                    consists of multiple rounds. Card text saying
                    "at the end of each turn" or "at the end of the
                    combat turn" refers to each combat round, not the
                    full game-turn.

    CONSUMABLE      A one-use item card. Must be equipped by an
                    entity before it can be used.

    CONTROL         Legal ownership of a fortress, independent of
                    whether it is currently occupied.

    DEFEAT          Reducing an entity's HP to 0.

    ENGAGEMENT      A single declared combat, resolved fully before
                    any other engagement begins.

    ENTITY          A unit card. Entities fight, move, equip items,
                    and capture fortresses.

    EQUIPMENT PHASE A Tactical Mode phase in which the active player
                    may equip items from their reserve hand to their
                    entities (no deck involved).

    EXTRA ACTION    An advanced rule (Section 20) allowing an entity
                    to "buy" an additional combat action by discarding
                    3 random cards from its controller's deck.

    FORTRESS        A structure card placed in the suburbs. Holds
                    entities and grants buffs. Can be captured or
                    destroyed.

    FORTRESS ASSAULT  A combat in which battlefield entities attack
                    a fortress. May target one or more enemy fortresses
                    in the same engagement.

    FORTRESS BARRAGE  An advanced combat type in which fortress
                    entities attack an enemy fortress directly, ship
                    to ship, without a battlefield assault.

    GAME TURN       The full 5-phase cycle a player takes: Card Play,
                    Combat, Movement, Card Draw, Victory Check. Not
                    to be confused with a Combat Round.

    GRAVEYARD       A player's discard pile. Each player has their
                    own.

    HAMLET          The loss-for-all end condition: no entities
                    remain and none can be played.

    HEALING         An advanced combat action (Section 18). The healer
                    rolls an attack; on hit, they discard a random card
                    from their deck and heal one entity in the
                    engagement by +1 HP.

    HIT POINTS (HP) A card's durability, tracked physically with a
                    die on top of the card.

    INITIAL VOLLEY  A one-shot attack fortress occupants can fire into
                    a real battlefield engagement before the first
                    combat round. Once per fortress per engagement;
                    resets at the start of each new engagement.

    ITEM            A card equipped to an entity, granting buffs,
                    debuffs, or special effects.

    LANDLORD        The end condition where one player controls all
                    fortresses in play with no more to come, and then
                    survives one full round without losing that
                    status.

    LAST STAND      An advanced rule allowing an entity at 0 HP to take
                    one final action before being discarded. If the
                    action raises the entity's HP back above 0 (e.g.,
                    via Heal), the entity survives.

    MEGA-ENGAGEMENT  A single engagement targeting multiple enemy
                    fortresses simultaneously. There is no cap on
                    the number of fortresses targeted in one
                    engagement.

    OCCUPY          Having one or more living entities physically
                    inside a fortress.

    PARRY           An advanced action canceling 1 HP of normal-hit
                    damage received on the previous round.

    RARE CARD       A card with a purple, orange, or yellow/gold
                    border.

    RETREAT         An advanced action to leave an engagement at the
                    cost of one round's action and exposure to an
                    enemy attack.

    SCAVENGER       An advanced rule allowing a player's surviving
                    entities to pick up their own dead allies' items
                    in an engagement. Items NEVER transfer between
                    different players' control.

    SHOP            An advanced face-up zone where players place 3
                    or 7 cards at setup (depending on deck regime)
                    for possible mid-game purchase.

    SUBURBS         The fortress zone at the edges of the table,
                    where fortresses are placed. The suburbs are the
                    battlefield for fortresses; the battlefield is
                    the battlefield for entities.

    SUPER ENTITY    A rare entity with 2 HP dice.

    SUPER FORTRESS  A rare fortress with 3 HP dice. (A normal
                    fortress has 2 HP dice.)



FAQ AND EDGE CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Q1. What if a player's hand has no legal plays and they must play
        3 cards?

        Discard cards until played + discarded = 3. The forced-play
        rule is only waived when the hand is entirely empty at the
        start of Card Play.

    Q2. What if a player's deck runs out during the Card Draw phase?

        Draw as many cards as possible. If only 1 card remains, keep
        it. If 0 cards remain, skip the Card Draw phase for the rest
        of the game. Play continues with the current hand until it is
        empty.

    Q3. Can a consumable's damage be parried?

        No. Parry only cancels 1 HP of damage from a normal attack.

    Q4. A fortress has three entities inside. Do they each get an
        Initial Volley shot?

        No. Each fortress gets ONE volley shot per engagement,
        regardless of how many entities are inside. The controlling
        player picks which occupant fires.

    Q5. A fortress is currently being assaulted. Can its occupants
        volley into a different battlefield engagement happening at
        the same time?

        No. A fortress under assault cannot fire an Initial Volley.
        Its occupants are already committed to defending their own
        fortress. The same restriction applies to fortresses
        participating in a Fortress Barrage.

    Q6. Both sides are destroyed in the same round. What happens?

        Both sides lose. If the engagement was a fortress assault
        and the fortress still stands, it remains under defender
        control (empty). If both sides were wiped on the
        battlefield, the engagement ends with no survivors on either
        side.

    Q7. May a player attack their own entities or fortresses?

        Not unless a card or scenario explicitly says so.

    Q8. Can items be held in a fortress without an entity?

        No. Items must be equipped to entities. If all entities in a
        fortress die and their items are not inherited (no Scavenger,
        no Last Stand transfer), the items are discarded.

    Q9. If a player purchases from the Shop and the bought card
         cannot be legally placed, what happens?

        It cannot be purchased. A Shop card must be playable on the
        spot.

    Q10. Can a card's HP be healed above its printed maximum?

        Only if a card explicitly says so. Otherwise healing caps at
        the printed value.

    Q11. A fortress was captured. When can its former occupants'
         leftover items be used?

        Never. Captured fortresses are empty of items by definition;
        all items owned by defeated defenders went to the defender's
        graveyard. The captor takes the real estate, not the
        inventory.

    Q12. A Supercharged Attack self-damaged the entity to 0. What
         happens?

        Last Stand triggers from any source of 0 HP, including
        self-damage from Supercharged Attack. The entity may take
        one final action before being discarded. If that action
        raises HP above 0 (e.g., a successful Heal), the entity
        survives.

    Q13. A card from Goosklerf I has Defense and the players are
         using pure GKII. Should Defense be ignored?

        Either apply the conversion rule from Part IV, or ignore
        Defense entirely and play the card as GKII. Decide before the
        game and stay consistent.

    Q14. Can a Super Entity be parried once per HP die, or only once
         per round like a normal entity?

        Parry is an action of the parrying entity, not the entity
        being parried. A Super Entity can parry one hit per round
        like any other entity. HP dice are irrelevant to parry.

    Q15. Can an entity at 0 HP use Healing as its Last Stand action
         to revive itself?

        Yes. Healing is a valid Last Stand action. On a successful
        heal roll, the entity's HP rises to 1 and it survives. On a
        miss, the entity is discarded normally.

    Q16. Is there a cap on how many Extra Actions an entity can buy
         in one round?

        No. Extra Actions are limited only by deck depth. Each costs
        3 randomly discarded cards. Stack as many as the deck allows.

    Q17. May enemy items ever be picked up via Scavenger?

        No. Scavenger only allows a player's surviving entities to
        pick up THEIR OWN dead allies' items. Items NEVER transfer
        between different players' control.

    Q18. Does destroying a Landlord's fortress break Landlord status?

        Not on its own. The Landlord still controls every remaining
        fortress, so status holds. The exception: if destruction
        leaves zero fortresses on the table, Landlord cannot be
        sustained and status ends.

    The following illustrations are referenced or implied throughout
    this rulebook. Each is listed with its purpose and the minimum
    information it must communicate. Illustrations should be
    functional first, stylish second. A player should be able to
    understand the thing from the illustration alone.

    FIGURE 1 - CARD ANATOMY
        One full page. Four card diagrams side by side (Entity,
        Fortress, Item, Consumable). Callouts label: border color,
        name, card-type icon, Attack stat, HP dice (note: normal
        entities have 1 die, super entities 2; normal fortresses 2
        dice, super fortresses 3), capacity (if any), text box, and
        any buff/debuff indicators. Show the fortress card with its
        buff/debuff text box clearly labeled, since fortress buffs
        apply to all occupants like a blanket modifier.
        Purpose: So a new player can identify every part of a card
        by glancing at the page.

        One full page. Top-down view of a 2-player Classic Mode

        player, each player's deck/hand/graveyard near them, optional
        shop column on one side. Label every zone. Use simple boxes,

        Half page. A flow diagram of the five phases (Card Play,

    GENERAL ART NOTES
        The setting of Goosklerf II is post-reality-collapse
        grimdark with absurd undertones. Key visual flavors for
        scenario illustrations: broken geometry, wrong animals
        in wrong places, humans who are almost human, concrete
        bureaucracy that has outlived its purpose, fluorescent
        light. The rulebook interior art should stay clean and
        functional; save the heavy mood for scenario chapter
        headers, card art, and the setting page.
