PAGE 38 - CAMPAIGN MODE: HEALING + SPECIAL ITEMS + CHARACTER SHEETS + MAP + MOVEMENT (28.8-28.12)


28.8 HEALING

Players may use a healing action via the advanced Healing rules
(Section 21). Alternatively, the DM may lay out special healing
zones on the map where players may restore some HP (such as in
a hospital).


28.9 SPECIAL ITEMS

Players equip normal item cards but may also equip up to 3
additional items invented by the DM with capabilities specified
by the DM. These invented items must be recorded on the
player's character sheet(s).


28.10 CHARACTER SHEETS

Full character stats are not limited by cards in Campaign Mode.
Stats, items, level, and other elements are recorded on each
entity's character sheet. A printable Campaign character sheet
is provided in the appendix.


28.11 MAP

Before the campaign, the DM either draws a game map or uses an
existing one. Maps should be drawn on grid paper. Entities
cannot move through boundaries unless allowed to via special
conditions by the DM. The map outlines where enemies, items,
fortresses, and other special gameplay elements lie. The DM
keeps track of where all gameplay elements are, ideally on a
parallel map: the players should be able to see the base map
(with fog of war optionally enabled by placing a sheet of paper
over part of the map) with fortresses and selected other
elements shown. When a player lands on a special square, an
event or combat is initiated or the entity picks up an item.

When in combat, allies in a 5x5 square around the battle party
may assist in combat. If an ally is too far away, they cannot
help.

Alternatively, players may play without a map and rely on
narrative only instead.


28.12 MOVEMENT

Each main character (with their party) occupies one grid space.
The battle party can move based on its character sheet stats,
which limit the maximum movement distance of a character. The
default maximum movement distance is 3 grid spaces. Players
roll a die to determine how far they were able to move on their
turn (they can move up to the maximum their roll allows, but
they do not need to move a maximum distance).

If a die rolls a 1, the character moves 1. If a die rolls 2,
the character moves 2, and so on. If the die rolls above the
movement limit, the result corresponds to the maximum movement
limit. For example, if a character can move 3 spaces max,
rolling a 6 means the player can move up to 3 grid spaces.
