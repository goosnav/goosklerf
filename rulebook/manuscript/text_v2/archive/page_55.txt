PAGE 55 - SCENARIO C: STATIONS FINISH + TIMER + ARGUMENTS


THE STOVETOP. Another burner, not the one the party woke
    up on. Still hot. The Sizzle rule applies here. The enemy
    at this station has +1 Attack (the heat makes everything
    angrier). But any entity defeated here is automatically
    arguable as "already cooked," which is a strong argument.

    THE FRIDGE. If the party reaches the fridge (requires 2
    rounds of travel instead of 1 — it is far from the
    stovetop), they find it open. Inside is cold and dark
    and full of old things. No combat. Each character may
    scavenge 2 items. Anything from the fridge is
    automatically arguable as edible (it was in the fridge,
    so at some point someone considered it food). But on a
    roll of 1 on 1d6 per item, the item is "expired." An
    expired ingredient can still be contributed to a course,
    but the creature will spit that course out during the
    tasting in Act IV (the DM reveals this only then, and
    the course does not count). Players do not learn which
    items are expired until the tasting.

THE TIMER IS VISIBLE. Remind the party of the timer at the
end of every round. They can hear it ticking. It is getting
louder.

ARGUING HAPPENS IN REAL TIME. When a player argues that an
entity is edible or that an item is a utensil, the argument
happens at the table, out loud, to the DM. Other players may
support or challenge the argument. The DM rules. This is the
scenario's signature mechanic and should be encouraged to be
theatrical. A player who stands up and passionately defends
the edibility of a shield boss should be rewarded for
commitment.

The party may designate items as serviceware at any point
during Acts II or III. Serviceware does not need to be argued
at the moment of acquisition. A player may hold an item and
decide later whether it is a weapon, an ingredient, or a
plate.
