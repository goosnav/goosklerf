30.5 LEVELING

    Characters level up at the end of each major act of the
    scenario, or whenever the DM decides the players have earned
    it.

        Level 2:  +1 maximum HP
        Level 3:  Once per session, reroll one failed roll
        Level 4:  +1 Attack (up to the maximum of 5)
        Level 5:  Squire Slot: a player may assign one extra entity
                  from their reserve as a squire. The squire holds
                  1 item and absorbs damage in the player's place:
                  when the player would take damage, they may
                  assign it to the squire first. If the squire dies,
                  the player loses it.
        Level 6:  +1 maximum HP
        Level 7:  +1 item capacity (4 instead of 3)
        Level 8+: Alternate between +1 HP, +1 reroll per session,
                  and one DM-approved custom perk.

30.6 STRUCTURE OF A SCENARIO

    Every official Campaign scenario is structured in 4 acts:

        Act I:    Entrance. Set the scene, establish the goal,
                  introduce the weird local rule.
        Act II:   Complication. A new threat or moral decision
                  raises the stakes.
        Act III:  Collapse. Pressure builds. Resources tighten.
                  Enemies escalate.
        Act IV:   Finale. Confront the core problem. Win, lose, or
                  make a costly choice.

    Between acts, characters may level up and (at the DM's
    discretion) rest to restore HP.

30.7 DEATH AND REVIVAL

    A character reduced to 0 HP is not dead unless the scenario
    says so. They are downed. At the end of the encounter, if
    their allies survive, the character recovers with 1 HP.

    If the character is downed during Act IV, or during a
    scripted "no second chances" beat, they die permanently.

    This is softer than standard Goosklerf II because Campaign
    Mode is about story, not attrition.

30.8 HEALING

    Players may use a healing action via the advanced Healing rules
    (Section 19). Alternatively, the DM may lay out special healing
    zones on the map where players may restore some HP (such as in
    a hospital).

30.9 SPECIAL ITEMS

    Players equip normal item cards but may also equip up to 3
    additional items invented by the DM with capabilities specified
    by the DM. These invented items must be recorded on the
    player's character sheet(s).

30.10 CHARACTER SHEETS

    Full character stats are not limited by cards in Campaign Mode.
    Stats, items, level, and other elements are recorded on each
    entity's character sheet.

    A printable Campaign character sheet is provided in the
    appendix.

30.11 MAP

    Before the campaign, the DM draws or selects a map on grid
    paper. The map shows where enemies, items, fortresses, and
    other special elements lie. The DM keeps a master copy with all
    elements marked (or tracks them by written coordinates), and
    reveals a player-facing version progressively, with fog of war
    optionally enabled by covering parts of the map. Entities
    cannot move through boundaries unless the DM allows it. When a
    character lands on a special square, an event or combat
    triggers, or the entity picks up an item.

    In combat, allies within a 5x5 square around the battle party
    may assist; allies farther away cannot.

    A campaign may be played without a map, using narrative only.

30.12 MOVEMENT

    Each main character (with their party) occupies one grid space.
    Roll 1d6; the character may move up to that many spaces, capped
    at their maximum movement (default 3). Rolls above the cap
    count as the cap. Movement is optional — a character may move
    less than the roll allows, or not at all.
