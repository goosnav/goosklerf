ACT II - THE COUNTER

Objective: Gather ingredients, serviceware, and weapons.

The countertop is a hostile environment. Everything here is
sized for someone who is not the party's size. A puddle of
olive oil is a lake. A dishrag is a landscape. A sponge is a
building with a disturbing texture.

The party explores the counter in search of things to fight,
things to cook, and things to cook with. The DM structures
the counter as a series of STATIONS. Each station costs 1
round of travel to reach.

At each station, TWO things happen:

    1. COMBAT. Pull 1 entity from the enemy pool. Combat is
       resolved normally. If the party defeats the entity,
       they may argue it as an ingredient (the DM judges the
       argument on the spot). If they cannot make a convincing
       case, the defeated entity is wasted. Not everything is
       food. Try harder next time.

    2. SCAVENGING. After combat (or instead of combat, if
       the party chooses to flee the station), each character
       at the station may spend 1 action to scavenge. Draw 1
       random item from the deck. The character keeps the item
       and may use it as equipment, or may immediately argue
       it as an ingredient or serviceware.

THE DM SHOULD FLAVOR EACH STATION. Suggested locations on
the counter for the DM to describe and improvise from:

    THE CUTTING BOARD. A scarred wooden plateau. Knife marks
    everywhere. Remnants of whatever was chopped here last.
    Good scavenging potential: items found here are more
    likely to be arguable as utensils or cutting implements.
    The entity here fights with sharp, chopping motions.

    THE SINK. A vast chrome basin with a dripping faucet the
    size of a waterfall. Wet. Slippery. Characters at the
    sink take -1 to attack rolls (bad footing). But any item
    scavenged here can be argued as "washed and therefore
    more plausibly edible," giving the player a +1 bonus to
    their edibility argument (DM should be more lenient).

    THE SPICE RACK. A vertical cliff of enormous jars. No
    combat at this station (it is peaceful here, somehow).
    Each character may scavenge 1 item. Any item found at
    the spice rack automatically counts as edible with no
    argument required. It is a spice. It came from the spice
    rack. That is the argument and it is unimpeachable. The
    DM may limit visits to the spice rack to once per
    scenario.

    THE TRASH CAN. The rim of a metal bin, overflowing with
    debris. 2 enemies at this station instead of 1 (things
    that live in garbage are territorial). Scavenged items
    here are harder to argue as edible (the DM should be
    stricter; "it came from the trash" is a fair counter-
    argument). But scavenging is plentiful: draw 2 items
    instead of 1.
